Title: Normal Bone X-ray Anatomy

# Normal Bone X-ray Anatomy: A Systematic Review

## General Principles of Bone X-ray Interpretation

All bone X-rays should be evaluated using the **ABCDEFG** approach:
- **A** — Alignment
- **B** — Bone density
- **C** — Cartilage (joint space)
- **D** — soft tissue **D**esign/details
- **E** — **E**piphyses (in children) / **E**dges
- **F** — Fractures
- **G** — Growth plates (pediatric)

## Normal Bone Density

Normal bone shows uniform, smooth cortical bone with:
- **Cortical bone**: Dense, white outer rim (compact bone)
- **Trabecular bone**: Fine, reticular internal pattern (cancellous/spongy bone)
- **Medullary cavity**: Central area, less dense, contains bone marrow

Signs of **abnormal bone density**:
- **Osteopenia**: Reduced bone density, appears more lucent
- **Osteoporosis**: Severe reduction in bone mass, cortical thinning, "pencil-line" appearance
- **Osteosclerosis**: Increased bone density, whiter than normal

## Joint Space Anatomy

Normal joint space should appear lucent (dark) on X-ray, representing articular cartilage:
- **Knee joint space**: 3–4 mm of cartilage (medial and lateral compartments)
- **Shoulder**: Glenohumeral joint space ~4 mm
- **Hip**: Acetabular and femoral head cartilage ~3–4 mm
- **Ankle**: Tibiotalar joint space ~3 mm

**Joint space narrowing** indicates cartilage loss (osteoarthritis).

## Normal Anatomical Variants

Common normal variants that may mimic disease:
- **Sesamoid bones**: Small bones within tendons (e.g., patella, foot sesamoids)
- **Accessory ossification centers**: Extra bone fragments in children that fuse later
- **Nutrient foramina**: Small lucencies in cortical bone from blood vessel channels
- **Mach lines**: Dense lines near joint margins that mimic fractures (common in knee)

## Knee X-ray Anatomy

### Standard Views
- **AP (Anteroposterior) view**: Shows femoral condyles, tibial plateaus, joint space
- **Lateral view**: Shows patella, suprapatellar pouch, quadriceps tendon
- **Skyline/Tangential view**: Tangential view of patella for patellofemoral assessment

### Key Landmarks
- **Medial and lateral femoral condyles**: Rounded distal femur structures
- **Medial and lateral tibial plateaus**: Flat proximal tibia weight-bearing surfaces
- **Intercondylar eminence (tibial spine)**: Central tibial projection
- **Fabella**: Sesamoid bone in lateral head of gastrocnemius (normal variant)

## Spine X-ray Anatomy

### Cervical Spine
- **Vertebral bodies**: C2–C7, increasing in size caudally
- **Intervertebral disc spaces**: Lucent spaces between vertebral bodies
- **Spinous processes**: Posterior bony projections
- **Intervertebral foramina**: Neural exit zones (visible on oblique views)

### Thoracolumbar Spine
- **12 thoracic vertebrae**: Associated with ribs
- **5 lumbar vertebrae**: Largest vertebral bodies, no ribs
- **Pedicles**: Bony pillars connecting body to posterior elements
- **Facet joints**: Zygapophyseal joints posteriorly
- **Sacroiliac joints**: Where sacrum meets ilium

## Long Bone Anatomy (Femur, Tibia, Humerus)

- **Diaphysis**: Shaft, thick cortical bone, narrow medullary canal
- **Metaphysis**: Widened ends near growth plates
- **Epiphysis**: End of bone, articulates with joint
- **Physis**: Growth plate (pediatric, radiolucent line)
- **Apophysis**: Bony projection for muscle/tendon attachment (not a joint surface)
