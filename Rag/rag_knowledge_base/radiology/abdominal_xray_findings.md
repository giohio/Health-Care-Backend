Title: Abdominal X-ray — Findings and Interpretation

# Abdominal X-ray: Systematic Interpretation

## Indications for Abdominal X-ray

- **Suspected bowel obstruction**: First-line imaging for suspected small or large bowel obstruction
- **Abdominal pain**: When CT is unavailable or contraindicated
- **Foreign body ingestion**: Initial imaging for radio-opaque objects
- **Kidney stones**: Some stones are radio-opaque (calcium-containing)
- **Constipation/impaction**: In pediatric and elderly patients
- **Perforation (suspected)**: Upright chest X-ray is better for free air

## Standard Views

- **Supine (AP) abdominal view**: Most common view, evaluates overall bowel gas pattern
- **Erect abdominal view**: Better for air-fluid levels and free air
- **Chest X-ray (upright)**: Best for detecting free subdiaphragmatic air

## Systematic Interpretation

### 1. Bowel Gas Pattern

**Normal bowel gas pattern**:
- Stomach: Air-fluid level in left upper quadrant
- Small bowel: Central, valvulae conniventes (circular folds) visible
- Large bowel: Peripheral, haustra (segmental indentations), Rectosigmoid in pelvis

**Abnormal patterns**:
- **Dilated small bowel**: >3 cm diameter, valvulae conniventes visible across lumen
- **Dilated large bowel**: >6 cm diameter (up to 9 cm in cecum), haustra visible
- **Disproportionate dilation**: Small bowel > colon suggests small bowel obstruction; colon > small bowel suggests large bowel obstruction
- **Absence of gas**: "Dead bowel" — may indicate complete obstruction or peritonitis

### 2. Air-Fluid Levels

Best seen on erect or decubitus views:
- Multiple air-fluid levels at different heights in same bowel loop = mechanical obstruction
- Long air-fluid levels (>2 cm) in dilated small bowel suggest obstruction
- "String of beads" sign: Dependent fluid with scattered gas bubbles (indicates small bowel obstruction)

### 3. Calcifications

Common calcifications on abdominal X-ray:

| Location | Likely Etiology |
|---|---|
| Right upper quadrant | Gallstones (20% radio-opaque) |
| Pelvis | Phleboliths (pelvic veins), common and benign |
| Mid-abdomen, bilateral | Renal artery calcification |
| Paravertebral | Aortic calcification (aneurysm screening) |
| Right lower quadrant | Appendicolith (calcified fecalith in appendix) |
| Upper abdomen | Pancreatic calcification (chronic pancreatitis) |
| Splenic region | Splenic artery calcification |

### 4. Soft Tissue Organ Silhouettes

- **Liver**: Right upper quadrant, smooth dome
- **Spleen**: Left upper quadrant
- **Kidneys**: Retroperitoneal, bilateral, upper abdomen
- **Psoas muscles**: Paravertebral, retroperitoneal fat planes
- **Bladder**: Pelvis (when filled with urine)

### 5. Free Air (Pneumoperitoneum)

Signs of bowel perforation:
- **Subdiaphragmatic free air**: Visible on upright chest/abdominal X-ray (best sensitivity with upright CXR)
- **Rigler sign (double wall sign)**: Air on both sides of bowel wall (visible when distended)
- **Falciform ligament sign**: Air outlining the falciform ligament
- **Cupola sign**: Air under the central diaphragm

### 6. Foreign Bodies

Radio-opaque objects on X-ray:
- **Metallic**: Coins, batteries, needles, glass, lead-containing objects
- **Bone**: Fish bones, chicken bones (most are visible)
- **Most plastics**: NOT visible on X-ray

## Common Pathological Patterns

### Small Bowel Obstruction
- Multiple dilated small bowel loops (>3 cm)
- Valvulae conniventes visible across lumen
- Air-fluid levels on erect view
- "String of beads" sign
- Collapse of colon and rectum (late finding)

### Large Bowel Obstruction
- Dilated large bowel (up to 9 cm in cecum)
- Haustra visible
- Often distal transition point visible
- Proximal small bowel dilation if ileocecal valve incompetent
- **Warning**: Cecum >9 cm risk of perforation

### Constipation/Impaction
- Fecal loading throughout colon
- "Ribbon-like" stool pattern
- Dilated rectum with fecal mass

### Appendicitis
- **Appendicolith**: Calcified fecalith in right lower quadrant (high specificity)
- **Sentinel loop**: Dilated small bowel in RLQ
- Loss of psoas margin (if retrocecal appendix)
