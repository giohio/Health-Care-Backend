# Fracture Classification: Radiological Principles

## General Fracture Terminology

### By Bone Continuity
- **Complete fracture**: Bone continuity fully disrupted
- **Incomplete fracture**: Bone continuity partially maintained (e.g., greenstick fracture in children)
- **Hairline fracture**: Very thin, nearly invisible fracture line

### By Fracture Line Direction
- **Transverse**: Perpendicular to the long axis of the bone
- **Oblique**: Angled fracture line (between 30-90 degrees to bone axis)
- **Spiral**: Curved or S-shaped fracture line (indicates rotational force)
- **Longitudinal**: Parallel to the long axis of the bone

### By Fragment Configuration
- **Simple fracture**: Two fragments only
- **Wedge fracture**: Three fragments (butterfly fragment)
- **Complex fracture**: More than three fragments
- **Comminuted fracture**: Multiple small fragments (high-energy trauma)

### By Soft Tissue Injury
- **Closed (simple) fracture**: Skin intact, no communication with external environment
- **Open (compound) fracture**: Skin breach, bone exposed or communicating wound
- **Grade I**: Puncture wound <1 cm, minimal contamination
- **Grade II**: Wound >1 cm without extensive soft tissue damage
- **Grade IIIA**: Extensive soft tissue damage, adequate coverage possible
- **Grade IIIB**: Extensive damage with periosteal stripping, requires flap coverage
- **Grade IIIC**: Associated with vascular injury requiring repair

## Displacement Classification

- **Varus/Valgus angulation**: Direction of distal fragment deviation
- **Translation (shift)**: Percentage of cortical width displaced
- **Shortening**: Overlap of fracture fragments causing limb shortening
- **Rotation**: Twisting of fragments around bone axis (difficult to assess on X-ray, requires clinical correlation)

## AO/OTA Fracture Classification

The AO Foundation classification is the gold standard for fracture description:

### Long Bone Segments
- **1** — proximal segment
- **2** — diaphyseal segment  
- **3** — distal segment

### Bone Codes
- **11** — Humerus proximal
- **12** — Humerus shaft
- **13** — Humerus distal
- **21** — Radius/Ulna proximal
- **22** — Radius/Ulna shaft
- **23** — Radius/Ulna distal
- **31** — Femur proximal
- **32** — Femur shaft
- **33** — Femur distal
- **41** — Tibia/Fibula proximal
- **42** — Tibia/Fibula shaft
- **43** — Tibia/Fibula distal

### Type Groups (Increasing Severity)
- **A — Extra-articular**: Does not involve the joint surface
- **B — Partial articular**: Some joint surface involvement but not complete
- **C — Complete articular**: Complete involvement of joint surface

### Subgroups
- **A1**: Simple, proximal
- **A2**: Wedge, proximal
- **A3**: Complex, proximal (comminuted)
- **B1**: Partial articular, split
- **B2**: Partial articular, depression
- **B3**: Partial articular, split-depression

## Key Fracture Patterns by Region

### Wrist and Forearm
- **Distal radius (Colles)**: Extra-articular, dorsal angulation
- **Distal radius (Smith)**: Extra-articular, volar angulation
- **Galeazzi fracture**: Radial shaft fracture + DRUJ dislocation
- **Monteggia fracture**: Ulna shaft fracture + radial head dislocation

### Ankle
- **Lauge-Hansen classification**: Based on foot position and mechanism
  - Supination-adduction (Stage I: fibula tip, Stage II: medial malleolus)
  - Supination-external rotation (Stage I-IV: progressively involving deltoid ligament to posterior malleolus)
  - Pronation-abduction (Stage I: medial malleolus transverse, Stage II: lateral malleolus comminuted)
  - Pronation-external rotation (Stage I-IV: most severe, all malleoli)

### Knee
- **Tibial plateau** (Schatzker classification):
  - Type I: Lateral split
  - Type II: Lateral split with depression
  - Type III: Pure depression (lateral)
  - Type IV: Medial plateau
  - Type V: Bicondylar
  - Type VI: Plateau with diaphyseal discontinuity
