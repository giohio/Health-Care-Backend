Title: X-ray vs CT vs MRI — Clinical Decision Guide

# Choosing the Right Imaging Modality: X-ray vs CT vs MRI

## Principle: ALARA

Always follow the **ALARA principle** (As Low As Reasonably Achievable):
- Start with the **lowest-radiation modality** that can answer the clinical question
- Progress to higher-radiation studies only if necessary
- MRI and ultrasound carry **no ionizing radiation**

## Radiation Dose Comparison

| Modality | Approximate Effective Dose (mSv) | Equivalent Natural Background |
|---|---|---|
| Chest X-ray (PA) | 0.02 | 1 day |
| Abdominal X-ray | 0.7 | 3 months |
| Mammogram | 0.4 | 7 weeks |
| Head CT | 2 | 1 year |
| Chest CT | 7 | 3 years |
| Abdominal CT | 10 | 4.5 years |
| Abdominal MRI | 0 | None |
| Brain MRI | 0 | None |

## Decision Framework by Body Region

### Head

| Clinical Question | First Choice | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Acute head trauma | **CT Head (non-contrast)** | — |
| Suspected stroke | **CT Head (non-contrast)** | MRI DWI |
| Chronic headache | MRI Brain | CT if MRI unavailable |
| Sinusitis | Clinical diagnosis | X-ray or CT (chronic) |
| Skull base pathology | CT Skull Base | MRI |

### Chest

| Clinical Question | First Choice | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Suspected pneumonia | **Chest X-ray** | — |
| Pulmonary nodule follow-up | **Chest X-ray** | CT for detailed assessment |
| Suspected lung cancer | **Chest X-ray** → CT chest | PET-CT for staging |
| Pulmonary embolism | **CT Pulmonary Angiography** | V/Q scan if contrast contraindicated |
| COPD/asthma | Chest X-ray | CT (severe disease) |
| Chest trauma | Chest X-ray first | CT (high-energy trauma) |

### Abdomen

| Clinical Question | First Choice | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Suspected bowel obstruction | **Abdominal X-ray** | CT abdomen for uncertainty |
| Suspected kidney stone | **Non-contrast CT KUB** | X-ray for follow-up |
| Appendicitis | **CT Abdomen** (contrast or non-contrast) | Ultrasound (pediatric) |
| Diverticulitis | **CT with contrast** | Ultrasound (mild cases) |
| Pancreatitis | **CT with contrast** | MRI for chronic pancreatitis |
| Liver lesion | MRI with contrast | CT arterial phase |

### Musculoskeletal

| Clinical Question | First Choice | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Suspected bone fracture | **X-ray** | — |
| Knee internal derangement | X-ray first | **MRI** (gold standard for meniscus/ACL) |
| Shoulder rotator cuff | X-ray first | **MRI** (gold standard) |
| Spinal cord compression | **MRI spine** | CT if MRI unavailable |
| Sacroiliac joints | X-ray first | **MRI** (early sacroiliitis) |
| Osteomyelitis | X-ray first | MRI (more sensitive) |
| Bone tumor | X-ray first | CT + MRI for staging |

## Special Populations

### Pediatric Patients
- **Prefer ultrasound** for abdominal pathology (appendicitis, intussusception)
- **Prefer MRI** over CT when possible (avoid radiation in growing bodies)
- **X-ray first** for suspected fractures
- Be aware of **increased sensitivity to radiation** in children

### Pregnant Patients
- **X-ray and CT carry radiation risk** — always document gestational age
- **Ultrasound and MRI (non-contrast) are preferred** during pregnancy
- X-ray of extremities: minimal fetal dose (<0.001 mSv)
- CT abdomen/pelvis: Significant fetal dose (~25 mSv) — avoid if possible
- **MRI with gadolinium**: Only if essential, limited data on safety

### Elderly Patients
- Higher baseline risk of osteoporosis — vertebral compression fractures more common
- X-ray may be insensitive for early or subtle fractures
- Consider MRI or CT for suspected hip fractures if X-ray negative but clinical suspicion high

## CT vs X-ray: When to Upgrade

Escalate from X-ray to CT when:
1. X-ray is negative but clinical suspicion remains high
2. Surgical planning requires more detail
3. Multiple injuries suspected (trauma panel)
4. Complex fracture patterns (e.g., acetabulum, calcaneus, pelvis)
5. Suspected visceral injury (liver, spleen, kidney)
6. Inadequate visualization due to patient body habitus

## Summary Decision Algorithm

```
Is it bone/joint?
├── Suspected fracture → X-ray first
│   └── Complex/navigation needed → CT
├── Suspected soft tissue injury → MRI
│
Is it chest?
├── Pneumonia/suspected lung lesion → Chest X-ray
│   └── Need detail/staging → CT chest
├── PE suspected → CT pulmonary angiography
│
Is it abdominal?
├── Bowel obstruction → Abdominal X-ray
│   └── Uncertain/detailed assessment → CT
├── Stone suspected → Non-contrast CT KUB
└── Appendicitis/intra-abdominal → CT abdomen
```
