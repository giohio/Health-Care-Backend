# Skull X-ray: Clinical Applications and Interpretation

## Role of Skull X-ray in Modern Practice

Skull X-ray has largely been replaced by CT scan for the evaluation of:
- **Acute head trauma**: CT is superior for detecting skull fractures and intracranial hemorrhage
- **Suspected intracranial pathology**: CT is faster and more sensitive

However, skull X-ray may still be used in specific settings:
- **Foreign body localization**: Radio-opaque foreign bodies in the skull/scalp
- **Pre-operative planning**: Assessing skull bone thickness before surgical procedures
- **Developing countries**: Where CT availability is limited
- **Screening**: Occupational health (e.g., industrial exposure)

## Standard Views

- **PA (posteroanterior) view**: Frontal bone, orbital roofs, nasal septum
- **Lateral view**: Both lateral walls, sella turcica, frontal bone
- **Townes view (AP axial)**: Occipital bone, foramen magnum, mastoid air cells
- **Submentovertex (basilar) view**: Base of skull, petrous pyramids

## Systematic Interpretation

### Skull Vault

Evaluate the **three layers** of the skull:
- **Outer table**: Outer cortical bone (dense white line)
- **Diploe**: Trabecular bone between tables (less dense)
- **Inner table**: Inner cortical bone (denser than diploe)

### Sutures

Normal cranial sutures in adults (fuse by age 30-40):
- **Coronal suture**: Between frontal and parietal bones
- **Sagittal suture**: Between two parietal bones (midline)
- **Lambdoid suture**: Between parietal and occipital bones
- **Squamous sutures**: Between temporal and parietal bones

**Premature suture closure** = craniosynostosis (in children)

### Fracture Patterns

**Linear skull fracture**: Most common, thin radiolucent line
- May cross suture lines (if fracture is more significant than suture)
- Most linear fractures are clinically insignificant if no neurological deficit

**Depressed skull fracture**: Fracture fragments displaced inward
- Bone fragments displaced > thickness of skull table = depressed
- May be comminuted
- Higher association with underlying brain injury
- CT preferred for assessing depression depth

**Basilar skull fracture**: Involves the base of skull
- **Anterior fossa**: CSF rhinorrhea (nose), Battle sign (raccoon eyes)
- **Middle fossa**: CSF otorrhea (ear), Battle sign (retroauricular ecchymosis)
- Often visible on skull X-ray; CT more sensitive

**Growing skull fracture (in children)**: Post-traumatic, follows dural tear
- Progressive enlargement of fracture line
- Typically involves parietal bone
- Associated with leptomeningeal cyst

### Sellar Region

The sella turcica houses the pituitary gland:
- **Normal sella**: Smooth contours, <10 mm depth
- **Empty sella**: Appears as widened sella with no bony destruction
- **Ballooned sella**: Enlarged sella with smooth remodeling (benign intracranial hypertension)
- **Destruction of sella**: Pituitary tumor, craniopharyngioma, metastases

### Foreign Bodies

Skull X-ray is useful for:
- **Retained radio-opaque foreign bodies** after penetrating trauma
- **Localization** for surgical planning
- **Bullet fragments**: Trajectory and location assessment
