Title: Spinal X-ray — Degenerative and Traumatic Findings

# Spinal X-ray: Interpretation of Degenerative and Traumatic Changes

## Standard Views

- **AP (Anteroposterior) view**: Vertebral body alignment, pedicles, spinous processes
- **Lateral view**: Disc space height, vertebral body alignment, vertebral body height
- **Oblique views**: Intervertebral foramina, facet joints, pars interarticularis
- **Flexion-extension views**: Assess for dynamic instability (spondylolisthesis)

## Systematic Interpretation

### Vertebral Body Anatomy

Each vertebral body should be evaluated for:
- **Height**: Normal, wedged (compression), or collapsed
- **Alignment**: Centrally positioned, no angulation
- **Margins**: Smooth cortical margins, no erosion
- **Density**: Normal bone density, no focal lytic or sclerotic lesions

### Disc Space Height

Normal disc spaces vary by region:
- **Cervical**: 3–5 mm
- **Thoracic**: 2–4 mm
- **Lumbar**: 5–15 mm (largest in L4-L5 and L5-S1)

**Disc space narrowing**: Loss of disc height indicates degenerative disc disease, typically at L4-L5 and L5-S1.

## Degenerative Changes

### Osteophyte Formation

Bony outgrowths from vertebral bodies:
- **Anterior osteophytes**: Most common, project anteriorly from vertebral body corners
- **Posterior osteophytes**: Can narrow spinal canal or intervertebral foramina
- **Syndectomy/bridging osteophytes**: Large osteophytes connecting adjacent vertebrae (seen in DISH, ankylosing spondylitis)

### Facet Joint Osteoarthritis

- **Joint space narrowing**: Loss of the facet joint cartilage
- **Subchondral sclerosis**: Increased density subchondral bone
- **Osteophyte formation**: Bony spurs at joint margins
- **Hypertrophy**: Enlarged, overgrown facet joints

### spondylosis (Cervical/Lumbar)

Degenerative changes affecting the discovertebral junction:
- **Disc space narrowing**
- **Endplate sclerosis**: Increased density of vertebral body endplates
- **Anterior osteophytes**
- **Uncovertebral joint hypertrophy** (cervical spine)
- **Radiculopathy** (nerve root compression from foraminal osteophytes)

## Alignment Assessment

### Cervical Spine Alignment

- **Normal cervical lordosis**: Gentle anterior convexity
- **Loss of cervical lordosis**: Can be positional or pathological (muscle spasm, ligament injury)
- **Kernig's/Bilateral facet dislocation**: Complete anterior displacement >50% of vertebral body width
- **Translation**: >3 mm or >20% = instability

### Scoliosis

Lateral curvature of spine:
- **Cobb angle**: Gold standard measurement; >10 degrees = scoliosis
- **Structural curve**: Does not fully correct on lateral bending
- **Non-structural (functional) curve**: Resolves with bending

### Spondylolisthesis

Anterior displacement of one vertebra over the one below:

**Meyerding Classification** (Grade I-IV):
- **Grade I**: 0-25% displacement
- **Grade II**: 25-50% displacement
- **Grade III**: 50-75% displacement
- **Grade IV**: 75-100% displacement
- **Spondyloptosis**: >100% (complete displacement off the vertebra below)

**Etiology**:
- **Isthmic (spondylolytic)**: Pars interarticularis defect (most common, lumbar spine)
- **Degenerative**: Facet joint and disc degeneration (older adults, L4-L5)
- **Traumatic**: Acute pars fracture
- **Dysplastic**: Congenital abnormality of the pars

## Traumatic Fractures

### Compression Fracture

- **Wedge compression**: Anterior vertebral body height loss, posterior elements intact
- **Biconcave (fish) vertebra**: Endplate depression, seen in osteoporosis
- **Burst fracture**: Involvement of posterior vertebral body wall (CT/MRI needed for canal compromise)

### Chance Fracture

Horizontal fracture through vertebral body, posterior elements, and ligamentous complex:
- **Mechanism**: Flexion-distraction (seatbelt injury)
- **Three-part injury**: Anterior column + posterior bony ligamentous injury
- **Associated with intra-abdominal injuries** (duodenum, pancreas, aorta)

### Jefferson Fracture (C1)

Burst fracture of C1 (atlas):
- **Bilateral lateral mass displacement**: On open-mouth odontoid view
- **Rule of Spence**: >7 mm combined lateral mass displacement suggests ligamentous disruption
- **Stability assessment**: Transverse ligament integrity (CT/MRI)

### Hangman's Fracture (C2)

Fracture of the C2 pars interarticularis:
- **Mechanism**: Hyperextension and distraction (judicial hanging mechanism, diving injury)
- **Effendi Classification**: Type I (stable) to Type III (unstable with facet dislocation)
- **Beware of concomitant C1-C2 instability**

## Disc Herniation

While disc herniations are best seen on MRI, X-ray may show:
- **Loss of disc space height**
- **Endplate irregularity**
- **Modic changes**: Endplate bone signal changes (Type I: edema, Type II: fat, Type III: sclerosis)
