# Radiology: Diagnostic Imaging in Clinical Practice

## What is Radiology?

Radiology is the medical specialty that uses imaging techniques to diagnose and sometimes treat diseases. The primary modalities include:

- **X-ray (Radiography)**: First-line imaging for bones, chest, and abdominal conditions. Quick, widely available, low cost.
- **Computed Tomography (CT)**: Cross-sectional imaging with higher detail. Used for trauma, stroke, pulmonary embolism, and complex abdominal imaging.
- **Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)**: Excellent soft tissue contrast. Used for brain, spinal cord, joints, and soft tissue tumors.
- **Ultrasound (US)**: Real-time, radiation-free imaging. First-line for obstetrics, abdominal, and vascular assessment.
- **Fluoroscopy**: Real-time moving X-ray images. Used for barium studies, catheter placement, and interventional procedures.

## Role of X-ray in Clinical Practice

X-ray remains the most frequently ordered imaging study worldwide due to its:
- **Speed**: Results available within minutes
- **Accessibility**: Available in almost every clinical setting
- **Cost-effectiveness**: Low relative cost compared to CT/MRI
- **Adequacy for many conditions**: First-line for suspected bone fractures, pneumonia, bowel obstruction

## When to Use X-ray vs Other Modalities

| Clinical Scenario | Preferred Modality |
|---|---|
| Suspected bone fracture | X-ray (first-line) |
| Suspected pneumonia | Chest X-ray (first-line) |
| Stroke evaluation | CT (non-contrast) or MRI (diffusion) |
| Suspected pulmonary embolism | CT pulmonary angiography |
| Knee internal derangement | MRI (gold standard), X-ray first |
| Bowel obstruction | Abdominal X-ray (first-line), CT for uncertainty |
| Spinal cord compression | MRI (gold standard) |
| Kidney stones | Non-contrast CT (gold standard), X-ray for follow-up |

## Clinical Decision Support for Imaging Orders

When ordering imaging, clinicians should:
1. Start with the simplest, lowest-radiation modality that can answer the clinical question
2. Consider the ALARA principle (As Low As Reasonably Achievable) for radiation exposure
3. Review prior imaging before ordering new studies
4. Correlate imaging findings with clinical presentation

## Key Radiological Terms

- **Opacification**: Increased density on X-ray, can indicate fluid, mass, or consolidation
- **Lucency**: Decreased density, can indicate air, fat, or gas
- **Silhouette sign**: Loss of normal border between structures due to adjacent opacity
- **Air bronchogram**: Visible air-filled bronchi against consolidated lung
- **Hyperinflation**: Increased lung volume, seen in COPD/asthma
- **Tracheal deviation**: Displacement of the trachea from midline
