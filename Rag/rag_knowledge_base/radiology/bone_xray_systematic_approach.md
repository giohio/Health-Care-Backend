Title: Systematic Approach to Bone and Joint X-ray Interpretation

# Systematic Approach to Bone and Joint X-rays

## The ABCDEFG Method

Always evaluate bone X-rays systematically:

### A — Adequacy and Alignment

**Adequacy**: Is the correct view obtained? Are all relevant structures included?
- Check if the joint above and below the injury are included
- Adequate penetration: bones should be visible without being overexposed

**Alignment**: Check for normal anatomical alignment:
- Long bones should be straight on the shaft view
- Joint surfaces should be parallel and congruent
- Check for angulation, displacement, or dislocation

**Alignment measurements**:
- **Valgus**: Outward angulation (knee, elbow)
- **Varus**: Inward angulation (knee)
- **Anterior/posterior translation**: Forward/backward displacement

### B — Bone Density

Evaluate whether bone density is normal:
- **Normal**: Uniform cortical thickness, visible trabecular pattern
- **Osteopenia**: Reduced density, cortical thinning
- **Osteoporosis**: Severe osteopenia, "codfish vertebrae" (compression fractures)
- **Osteosclerosis**: Increased density, whiter than expected
- **Lytic lesion**: Localized area of bone destruction
- **Sclerotic lesion**: Localized area of increased bone density

### C — Cartilage (Joint Space)

Joint space represents articular cartilage:
- **Normal joint space**: 3–5 mm depending on joint
- **Narrowed space**: Cartilage loss (osteoarthritis, inflammatory arthritis)
- **Widened space**: Effusion, hemarthrosis, or ligamentous injury
- **Joint effusion**: Visible soft tissue density within joint capsule

### D — Soft Tissue

Always examine soft tissues surrounding the bone:
- **Soft tissue swelling**: Indicates trauma, infection, or inflammation
- **Fat planes**: Obliteration of normal fat planes indicates underlying pathology
- **Gas in soft tissue**: Trauma, infection (gas-forming organisms), or recent surgery
- **Foreign bodies**: Radio-opaque objects visible on X-ray

### E — Epiphyses (Children) / Edges

**In pediatric X-rays**:
- Check epiphyses for size, shape, and position
- Growth plates (physes) should be smooth and regular
- Compare with expected bone age

**In adults**:
- Check articular margins for erosions, osteophytes
- Check edges for cortical breaks

### F — Fractures

Systematically search the entire bone for fracture lines:
- **Transverse fracture**: Perpendicular to bone axis
- **Oblique fracture**: Angled fracture line
- **Spiral fracture**: Spiral/curved fracture (mechanism: rotational force)
- **Comminuted fracture**: Multiple bone fragments
- **Impacted fracture**: Fragments driven into each other
- **Avulsion fracture**: Small fragment pulled away by tendon/ligament attachment
- **Stress fracture**: Hairline fracture from repetitive stress (athletic, insufficiency)
- **Pathological fracture**: Fracture through abnormal bone (tumor, cyst, osteoporosis)

### G — Growth Plates (Pediatric)

Growth plates in children appear as transverse lucent lines:
- **Salter-Harris classification**: Classifies physeal fractures by involvement of epiphysis, physis, and metaphysis
  - Type I: Through the physis (separation)
  - Type II: Through physis and metaphysis (most common)
  - Type III: Through physis and epiphysis
  - Type IV: Through epiphysis, physis, and metaphysis
  - Type V: Crush injury to physis (most difficult to diagnose)

## Common Fracture Patterns

### Wrist and Hand
- **Colles fracture**: Distal radius dorsal angulation (fall on outstretched hand)
- **Smith fracture**: Distal radius volar angulation (reverse Colles)
- **Scaphoid fracture**: Most common carpal bone fracture; look for tenderness in anatomical snuffbox
- **Bennett fracture**: Thumb metacarpal base intra-articular fracture-dislocation

### Shoulder
- **Anterior shoulder dislocation**: Humeral head displaced anteriorly, Hill-Sachs deformity on humeral head
- **Clavicle fracture**: Most common pediatric fracture; middle third most common site
- **Proximal humerus fracture**: Surgical, anatomical, greater tuberosity anatomical neck classification

### Knee
- **Tibial plateau fracture**: Depression of weight-bearing tibial surface (LOCO classification)
- **Patellar fracture**: Direct trauma or forceful quadriceps contraction
- **Segond fracture**: Lateral tibial plateau avulsion (ACL injury marker)

### Ankle
- **Weber classification** (lateral malleolus fractures):
  - Type A: Below the syndesmosis (usually stable)
  - Type B: At the syndesmosis (may be stable)
  - Type C: Above the syndesmosis (usually unstable)
- **Maisonneuve fracture**: Proximal fibula fracture with ankle injury (high-energy)

### Hip and Femur
- **Femoral neck fracture**: Garden classification (I-IV); intracapsular, risk of AVN
- **Intertrochanteric fracture**: Extracapsular, between greater and lesser trochanters
- **Subtrochanteric fracture**: Below lesser trochanter
