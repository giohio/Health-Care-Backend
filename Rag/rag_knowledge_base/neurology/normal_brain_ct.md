Title: Normal Brain CT Anatomy

# Normal Brain CT: Systematic Interpretation

## Introduction

Non-contrast CT of the brain is the first-line imaging modality in emergency neurology due to:
- Speed (acquired in seconds)
- Wide availability (24/7 in most hospitals)
- Excellent sensitivity for acute hemorrhage, bone, and calcification
- Ability to detect mass effect and midline shift

## Windows and Levels

CT interpretation requires adjusting window settings:
- **Brain window** (W: 80, L: 35–40 HU): Default brain parenchyma
- **Subdural window** (W: 200–300, L: 30–50 HU): Better for detecting subtle subdural collections
- **Bone window** (W: 1500–3000, L: 300–600 HU): Skull, fractures, calcifications

## Systematic Interpretation

### 1. Midline Structures

**Midline** should be assessed first:
- **Falx cerebri**: Midline dural reflection between cerebral hemispheres
- **Third ventricle**: Central, slit-like structure between thalami
- **Fourth ventricle**: Posterior fossa, between cerebellum and brainstem

**Midline shift**: Displacement of midline structures >5 mm indicates significant mass effect.

### 2. Basal Cisterns

- **Suprasellar cistern**: Surrounds pituitary stalk, contains Circle of Willis
- **Sylvian fissures**: Lateral, bilaterally symmetric
- **Quadrigeminal cistern**: Posterior, above superior cerebellar vermis

**Compressed or absent basal cisterns** = elevated intracranial pressure, emergency.

### 3. Gray and White Matter Differentiation

**Normal**:
- Gray matter: Hyperdense (brighter) than white matter
- White matter: Hypodense (darker) than gray matter
- Clear distinction between cortical ribboning and underlying white matter

**Abnormal**:
- Loss of gray-white differentiation = early ischemia, edema, or mass
- "DenseMCA sign" = hyperdense middle cerebral artery (acute thrombus)

### 4. Ventricles

- **Lateral ventricles**: Symmetric, fluid-filled spaces
- **Third ventricle**: Central, narrow
- **Fourth ventricle**: Posterior fossa, between cerebellum and pons

**Enlarged ventricles**:
- **Obstructive hydrocephalus**: Dilated ventricles proximal to obstruction
- **Communicating hydrocephalus**: All ventricles dilated, no obstruction identified
- **Ex vacuo**: Ventricular enlargement from brain atrophy (elderly, neurodegenerative disease)

### 5. Sulci and Gyri

- **Symmetric sulcal pattern**: Finger-like CSF spaces between gyri
- **Prominent sulci**: Brain atrophy (elderly, dementia)
- **Effaced/absent sulci**: Cerebral edema, mass effect, meningitis

## Density Interpretation

| Density | Appearance | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Hyperdense** (white) | Blood, bone, contrast, calcification | Acute hemorrhage, calcified tumor |
| **Isodense** (gray) | Proteinaceous fluid, some tumors | Subacute hemorrhage, some tumors |
| **Hypodense** (black/dark) | CSF, water, fat, air, ischemia | CSF, edema, infarct, cysts |
| **Mixed density** | Various tissues | Tumor, abscess, contusion |

## Acute Findings on CT

### Acute Hemorrhage
- **Hyperdense** (bright white), well-defined
- Acute blood: 50-70 HU
- Surrounding hypodensity (edema) develops over hours
- Mass effect with ventricular compression

### Acute Ischemic Stroke
- **Early signs** (0-6 hours):
  - Loss of insular ribbon
  - Obscuration of lentiform nucleus
  - Dense MCA sign
  - Loss of sulci
- **CT Angiography**: Identifies occluded vessel, collateral circulation

### Brain Tumors
- **High-grade glioma**: Irregular, heterogeneous, ring enhancement
- **Meningioma**: Dural-based, calcified, homogeneous enhancement
- **Metastases**: Multiple, well-defined, at gray-white junction

### Cerebral Edema
- **Cytotoxic edema**: Loss of gray-white differentiation (ischemia)
- **Vasogenic edema**: White matter hypodensity, spares cortex (tumors, infection)
- **Diffuse cerebral edema**: Effaced sulci and ventricles, slit-like ventricles
