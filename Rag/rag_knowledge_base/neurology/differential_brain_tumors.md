# Brain Tumour Differential Diagnosis

## Intra-axial vs Extra-axial Distinction

The first step when approaching a brain mass on MRI is determining whether it is intra-axial (within brain parenchyma) or extra-axial (outside brain parenchyma).

| Feature | Intra-axial | Extra-axial |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Within brain parenchyma | Outside brain, within skull |
| Grey-white junction | Crosses or destroys it | Displaces inward |
| CSF cleft sign | Absent | Present (CSF rim around mass) |
| Dural attachment | No | Often present (dural tail) |
| Examples | Glioma, metastasis, lymphoma | Meningioma, acoustic neuroma |

## Intra-axial Tumour Differentials

### 1. Glioblastoma (GBM) — WHO Grade 4

The most common primary malignant brain tumour in adults.

**Key MRI features:**
- Thick irregular ring enhancement on post-contrast T1
- Central necrosis within the enhancing rim
- Extensive T2/FLAIR hyperintensity (infiltrative oedema)
- Crosses corpus callosum — butterfly glioma pattern
- Rapid progression on serial imaging

**Typical patient:** Adult over 50 years with no prior malignancy

### 2. Brain Metastasis

The most common intracranial tumour overall.

**Key MRI features:**
- Multiple lesions at grey-white matter junction (most specific finding)
- Well-defined thin ring or solid enhancement
- Disproportionately large vasogenic oedema relative to lesion size
- Haemorrhagic tendency: melanoma, RCC, choriocarcinoma, thyroid

**Most common primary tumours by frequency:**
- Lung cancer (most common, especially small cell)
- Breast cancer
- Melanoma
- Renal cell carcinoma
- Colorectal cancer

### 3. Primary CNS Lymphoma (PCNSL)

**Key MRI features:**
- Homogeneous solid enhancement (NOT ring enhancement — key distinction from GBM)
- Periventricular or deep white matter location
- Diffusion restriction on DWI — low ADC (hypercellular tumour)
- Associated with immunosuppression: HIV, post-transplant, elderly immunocompetent
- Dramatic response to corticosteroids — tumour may disappear (diagnostic clue)

### 4. Low-Grade Glioma — WHO Grade 2

**Key MRI features:**
- No or minimal contrast enhancement (unlike high-grade glioma)
- T2/FLAIR hyperintense, well-circumscribed margins
- Slow growth, may contain calcification
- Frontal or temporal lobe location in young adults aged 20–40 years

### 5. Brain Abscess (mimics ring-enhancing tumour)

**Key MRI features:**
- Rim enhancement with central diffusion restriction — bright DWI, low ADC (pus)
- Clinical: fever, elevated WBC, immunosuppression, dental procedure, IV drug use
- DWI is the critical distinguishing sequence: abscess restricts, GBM necrosis does not

## Comparison Table: Key Differentiators

| Feature | GBM | Metastasis | Lymphoma |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enhancement pattern | Thick irregular ring | Thin ring or solid | Homogeneous solid |
| Location | Any lobe, crosses midline | Grey-white junction | Periventricular |
| Number of lesions | Usually single | Often multiple | Single or multiple |
| Central necrosis | Yes | Rare | Absent |
| DWI/ADC | Variable | Variable | Restricted (low ADC) |
| Oedema | Extensive infiltrative | Disproportionate | Moderate |
| Patient profile | >50 years | Known primary cancer | Immunosuppressed or elderly |

## Approach by Number of Lesions

**Single lesion, adult over 50:**
- GBM vs single metastasis vs abscess
- Clinical history of prior malignancy is most important distinguishing factor

**Multiple lesions:**
- Metastases most likely (especially with known primary cancer)
- CNS lymphoma (periventricular, homogeneous enhancement)
- Gliomas are rarely multifocal — multiple lesions strongly favour metastases

## Essential MRI Sequences for Brain Tumours

| Sequence | Clinical Purpose |
|---|---|
| T1 post-contrast (gadolinium) | Enhancement pattern, blood-brain barrier breakdown |
| T2/FLAIR | Oedema extent, infiltration, white matter changes |
| DWI/ADC | Diffusion restriction — abscess vs lymphoma vs tumour necrosis |
| T2*/SWI | Microhaemorrhage, calcification, haemorrhagic metastases |
| T1 pre-contrast | Haemorrhage, calcification, fat, baseline anatomy |

## Summary

- Glioblastoma: thick ring, necrosis, crosses midline, >50 years
- Metastasis: grey-white junction, multiple, large oedema, known primary
- Lymphoma: solid homogeneous, periventricular, DWI restriction, steroid-sensitive
- Abscess: restricted diffusion in centre (pus), clinical infection signs
