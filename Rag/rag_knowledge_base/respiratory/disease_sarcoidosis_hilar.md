# Pulmonary Sarcoidosis — Bilateral Hilar Lymphadenopathy

## Overview

Sarcoidosis is a systemic granulomatous disease of unknown aetiology that most commonly affects the lungs and intrathoracic lymph nodes. Pulmonary sarcoidosis accounts for >90% of cases. The hallmark chest X-ray (CXR) finding is **bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy (BHL)**, which is symmetric and described as "potato nodes" bilaterally.

## Epidemiology

- Peak incidence: 20–40 years old
- More common in Black Americans and Northern Europeans (Scandinavians)
- Female > Male (slightly)

## CXR / Radiographic Staging (Scadding Classification)

| Stage | Description | Prevalence |
|-------|-------------|-----------|
| 0 | Normal chest radiograph | 5–10% |
| I | **Bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy** only | 40–50% |
| II | Bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy + pulmonary infiltrates | 30–40% |
| III | Pulmonary infiltrates without hilar lymphadenopathy | 10–15% |
| IV | Pulmonary fibrosis (end-stage) | 5% |

**Key teaching point**: Bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy on chest X-ray in an asymptomatic young adult is sarcoidosis until proven otherwise.

## Bilateral Hilar Lymphadenopathy — Differential Diagnosis

When bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy is found on CXR:

1. **Sarcoidosis** (most common in young adults, symmetric BHL)
2. Lymphoma (asymmetric, constitutional symptoms — night sweats, weight loss)
3. Primary tuberculosis (TB) — unilateral or asymmetric, Ghon focus may be present
4. Pulmonary hypertension — enlarged central pulmonary arteries (not true lymphadenopathy)
5. Metastatic malignancy (less common, known primary)

**Poncet's disease** (reactive arthritis + sarcoidosis) triad: bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy + erythema nodosum + ankle periarthritis (Lofgren syndrome — good prognosis).

## Clinical Features

- Often asymptomatic, found incidentally on CXR
- Dry cough, dyspnoea, chest pain (pleuritic)
- Constitutional: fatigue, fever, weight loss
- Extra-pulmonary: uveitis, skin (lupus pernio, erythema nodosum), peripheral neuropathy, hypercalcaemia, cardiac arrhythmia

## Pulmonary Function Tests (PFTs)

- Restrictive pattern: reduced FVC, reduced TLC
- Reduced DLCO (diffusion capacity) — sensitive marker of pulmonary sarcoidosis
- FEV1/FVC ratio normal or increased

## CT Chest Findings in Pulmonary Sarcoidosis

- **Perilymphatic nodules** — along bronchovascular bundles, interlobular septa, and fissures
- **Alveolar sarcoidosis** — confluent consolidation, "galaxy sign"
- **Hilar and mediastinal lymphadenopathy** — bilateral, symmetric
- Upper and middle lobe predominance
- "Egg-shell calcification" in chronic lymph nodes

## Diagnosis

- **Serum ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme)** — elevated in 60% (not specific)
- **Serum calcium** — hypercalcaemia (granuloma-mediated 1-hydroxylation of vitamin D)
- **BAL (bronchoalveolar lavage)**: lymphocytosis, CD4:CD8 ratio >3.5
- **Tissue biopsy**: non-caseating granulomas (essential for definitive diagnosis)
- **EBUS-TBNA** (endobronchial ultrasound-guided transbronchial needle aspiration) — first-line for sampling hilar/mediastinal nodes

## Management

- Stage I/II asymptomatic: observation (60–70% spontaneous remission)
- Symptomatic or progressive: **oral corticosteroids** (prednisolone 20–40 mg/day, taper over 6–12 months)
- Refractory: methotrexate, azathioprine, hydroxychloroquine
- Pulmonary hypertension and end-stage fibrosis: lung transplantation

## Prognosis

- Stage I: >90% remission
- Stage II: ~70% remission
- Stage III–IV: progressive fibrosis in 10–20%
- Chronic sarcoidosis (>2 years): higher risk of fibrosis
