# Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP)

## Definition

Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) is an acute infection of the pulmonary parenchyma acquired outside of a hospital. It is a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide.

## Etiology

**Typical organisms:**
- *Streptococcus pneumoniae* — most common bacterial cause
- *Haemophilus influenzae* — common in COPD patients
- *Staphylococcus aureus* — post-influenza pneumonia, cavitating lesions

**Atypical organisms:**
- *Mycoplasma pneumoniae* — young adults, walking pneumonia
- *Chlamydophila pneumoniae* — gradual onset, hoarseness
- *Legionella pneumophila* — hyponatraemia, high fever, contaminated water

**Viral causes:** Influenza A/B, SARS-CoV-2, RSV, adenovirus

## Clinical Features

**Symptoms:** Fever, rigors, productive cough with purulent or rusty sputum, pleuritic chest pain, dyspnoea, confusion in elderly

**Signs:** Tachycardia, tachypnoea, dullness to percussion, bronchial breathing, coarse crackles, increased vocal fremitus

## Chest X-Ray Patterns in Pneumonia

- Lobar or segmental consolidation — typical bacterial pneumonia
- Bilateral patchy infiltrates — atypical or viral pneumonia
- Cavitation — Staph aureus, Klebsiella, anaerobes
- Pleural effusion — parapneumonic effusion

## CURB-65 Severity Score

Score 1 point for each criterion:
- **C** — Confusion (new onset)
- **U** — Urea >7 mmol/L
- **R** — Respiratory rate ≥30/min
- **B** — Blood pressure: systolic <90 or diastolic ≤60 mmHg
- **65** — Age ≥65 years

| CURB-65 Score | Mortality Risk | Management |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | Low (<3%) | Outpatient oral antibiotics |
| 2 | Moderate (9%) | Consider hospital admission |
| 3–5 | High (15–40%) | Hospital admission, consider ICU |

## Antibiotic Treatment

**Mild CAP (CURB-65 0–1, outpatient):**
- Amoxicillin 500 mg TDS orally x 5 days (first-line, typical organisms)
- Add clarithromycin 500 mg BD x 5 days if atypical suspected
- Penicillin allergy: doxycycline 200 mg loading then 100 mg OD x 5 days

**Moderate CAP (CURB-65 2, hospital admission):**
- Co-amoxiclav 625 mg TDS + clarithromycin 500 mg BD x 7 days
- Step down to oral at 48 hours if clinically improving

**Severe CAP (CURB-65 3–5, HDU/ICU):**
- Co-amoxiclav 1.2 g TDS IV + clarithromycin 500 mg BD IV x 10–14 days
- Legionella suspected: levofloxacin 500 mg BD IV
- Post-influenza Staph aureus: add flucloxacillin 1–2 g QDS IV

**Atypical CAP (Mycoplasma, Legionella):**
- Clarithromycin 500 mg BD or doxycycline
- Legionella: levofloxacin or azithromycin

## Admission Criteria

Admit to hospital if any of:
- CURB-65 score ≥ 2
- SpO2 < 94% on room air
- Bilateral or multilobar consolidation on CXR
- Unable to tolerate oral medication

## Complications

- Parapneumonic effusion or empyema
- Lung abscess (anaerobes, Staph aureus)
- Septicaemia, septic shock
- Respiratory failure requiring mechanical ventilation

## Follow-up

- Repeat CXR at 6 weeks to confirm resolution
- Persistent opacity: consider malignancy, TB, organising pneumonia
- Pneumococcal vaccine and annual influenza vaccine post-recovery
