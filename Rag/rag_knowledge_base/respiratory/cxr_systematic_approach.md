# Systematic Chest X-Ray Reading — ABCDE Approach

## Technical Adequacy First

Before interpreting any chest x-ray, assess technical quality:
- **Projection**: PA (posteroanterior) is standard; AP magnifies mediastinum
- **Rotation**: Medial clavicle ends equidistant from spinous process
- **Inspiration**: 6 anterior ribs visible above diaphragm in full inspiration
- **Exposure**: Vertebral disc spaces just visible through cardiac shadow

## A — Airway

- Trachea should be midline — deviation indicates tension pneumothorax, collapse, or mediastinal mass
- Carina angle normally less than 70 degrees — widening suggests left atrial enlargement
- Both main bronchi should be visible
- Check for foreign bodies in airways

## B — Breathing (Lung Fields)

Review both lung fields systematically zone by zone (upper, mid, lower).

| Pattern | Appearance on CXR | Common Causes |
|---|---|---|
| Consolidation | Homogeneous opacity, air bronchogram visible | Pneumonia, pulmonary oedema |
| Collapse (atelectasis) | Volume loss, displaced fissures | Obstruction, post-surgical |
| Pleural effusion | Meniscus sign, blunted costophrenic angle | Heart failure, infection, malignancy |
| Pneumothorax | Visible pleural edge, absent lung markings peripherally | Trauma, spontaneous |
| Ground-glass opacity | Hazy increased density, vessels still visible | Pulmonary oedema, early pneumonia |
| Cavitation | Air-containing space within opacity | Abscess, TB, malignancy |

## C — Cardiac

- Cardiothoracic ratio: cardiac width should be less than 50% of thoracic width on PA film
- Cardiomegaly: CTR greater than 50% — suggests cardiac failure or pericardial effusion
- Normal left border: aortic knuckle, pulmonary artery knuckle, left ventricle
- Double density sign indicates left atrial enlargement

## D — Diaphragm

- Right hemidiaphragm normally 1.5–2.5 cm higher than left due to liver
- Both costophrenic angles should be acute — blunting indicates at least 200 mL fluid
- Free subdiaphragmatic air indicates bowel perforation
- Raised hemidiaphragm: phrenic nerve palsy, collapse, hepatomegaly

## E — Everything Else

**Bones:** Rib fractures (check posterior arcs), lytic or sclerotic lesions, vertebral compression fractures

**Soft tissues:** Surgical emphysema, mastectomy causing asymmetric translucency, pacemaker/ICD leads, central venous catheters, chest drains

**Mediastinum:**
- Superior widening greater than 8 cm: aortic dissection, lymphadenopathy, masses
- Anterior mediastinal masses — the 4 Ts: Thymoma, Teratoma, Terrible lymphoma, Thyroid
- Posterior mediastinal masses: neurogenic tumours, descending aorta pathology

## Common CXR Pattern Summary

| CXR Finding | Key Feature | Likely Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| Right lower zone opacity with air bronchogram | Homogeneous, lobar | Lobar pneumonia |
| Bilateral perihilar haze | Bat-wing distribution | Pulmonary oedema |
| White-out with trachea deviated away | Massive effusion | Malignant pleural effusion |
| White-out with trachea deviated toward | Volume loss | Lobar collapse |
| Peripheral lucency, absent lung markings | Visible pleural edge | Pneumothorax |
| Upper lobe fibrosis, hilar retraction | Tracheal deviation toward lesion | Tuberculosis |
| Patchy bilateral infiltrates | Diffuse airspace opacity | Viral or atypical pneumonia |

## Structured Reporting Template

1. Technical adequacy (projection, rotation, inspiration, exposure)
2. Airway and mediastinum
3. Lung fields (compare left to right, zone by zone)
4. Cardiac size and borders
5. Diaphragm and costophrenic angles
6. Bones and soft tissues
7. Conclusion and differential diagnosis
