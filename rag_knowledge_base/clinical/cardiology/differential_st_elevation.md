# ST Elevation — Differential Diagnosis

## Definition

ST elevation: ≥ 1 mm (0.1 mV) above baseline in 2 contiguous limb leads, or ≥ 2 mm in 2 contiguous precordial leads.

## Life-Threatening Causes (URGENT)

### 1. STEMI (ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction)
- **Pattern:** Convex/tombstone ST elevation with reciprocal ST depression in opposite leads
- **Localisation:**

| Territory | Leads with STE | Culprit Artery |
|---|---|---|
| Anterior | V1–V4 | LAD |
| Inferior | II, III, aVF | RCA (80%) or LCx |
| Lateral | I, aVL, V5–V6 | LCx |
| Posterior | ST depression V1–V3 + tall R wave | RCA or LCx |
| Right ventricle | V4R (best), III > II STE | Proximal RCA |

- **Reciprocal changes** strongly favour STEMI over benign causes
- **LBBB + STEMI criteria (Sgarbossa):** STE ≥ 1 mm concordant with QRS; ST depression V1–V3; STE ≥ 5 mm discordant (less specific)

### 2. Aortic Dissection
- Tearing chest pain radiating to back, BP differential between arms
- Can cause STEMI if dissection involves coronary ostia (inferior most common)
- **Do NOT give thrombolytics** — CT aortogram first

### 3. Takotsubo Cardiomyopathy
- Emotional/physical trigger, post-menopausal women, apical ballooning on echo
- Diffuse STE or deep T-wave inversions, usually transient

## Benign Causes

### Early Repolarisation (ER)
- Concave-up (smiley face) ST elevation, often V2–V5
- J-point notching, slurred ST takeoff
- Seen in young athletic males
- Generally benign, but high-risk ER (inferior/lateral + horizontal ST) linked to VF

### Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH)
- STE in V1–V2 (right precordial leads) with LBBB-pattern repolarisation
- Reciprocal ST depression V5–V6

### Pericarditis
- **Diffuse** concave STE in multiple non-contiguous territories
- PR segment depression (highly specific — opposite to STE)
- No reciprocal changes (except aVR)
- Saddle-shaped pattern
- 4 stages: STE (Stage 1) → ST normalises → T-wave inversion → normalisation

### Brugada Syndrome
- Coved-type STE (Type 1) in V1–V2: ≥ 2 mm STE with downsloping to inverted T
- Risk of VF/sudden death
- Exacerbated by fever, sodium channel blockers

### Hyperkalemia
- Peaked T waves first, then QRS widening, sine wave pattern
- Can mimic STE

## Practical Approach to STE

1. Is patient haemodynamically stable?
2. Any high-risk features? (chest pain, diaphoresis, syncope)
3. Contiguous distribution? (STEMI) vs diffuse? (pericarditis)
4. Reciprocal changes? (STEMI: yes; pericarditis: rarely)
5. PR depression? (pericarditis)
6. Compare with prior ECG
7. Serial ECGs and troponin if uncertain

## Priority Level: EMERGENCY if STEMI suspected — target door-to-balloon < 90 min
