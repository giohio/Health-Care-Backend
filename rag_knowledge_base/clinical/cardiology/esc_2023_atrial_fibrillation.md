# ESC 2023 Guidelines on Atrial Fibrillation (AF)

## AF Classification

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| First-diagnosed AF | First episode, regardless of duration/symptoms |
| Paroxysmal AF | Self-terminating, usually < 48 h (max 7 days) |
| Persistent AF | Sustained > 7 days, requires cardioversion |
| Long-standing persistent AF | Continuous AF > 12 months |
| Permanent AF | Accepted by patient and physician; no rhythm control |

## Stroke Risk Stratification — CHA₂DS₂-VASc Score

| Risk Factor | Score |
|---|---|
| C — Congestive heart failure | 1 |
| H — Hypertension | 1 |
| A₂ — Age ≥ 75 years | 2 |
| D — Diabetes mellitus | 1 |
| S₂ — Stroke/TIA/thromboembolism | 2 |
| V — Vascular disease (MI, PAD, aortic plaque) | 1 |
| A — Age 65–74 years | 1 |
| Sc — Sex category (female) | 1 |
| **Maximum** | **9** |

**Anticoagulation threshold:**
- Men: CHA₂DS₂-VASc ≥ 1 → offer OAC; ≥ 2 → recommend OAC
- Women: CHA₂DS₂-VASc ≥ 2 → offer OAC; ≥ 3 → recommend OAC

## Oral Anticoagulation (OAC)

- **Preferred:** NOACs (DOACs) — apixaban, rivaroxaban, dabigatran, edoxaban
  - Apixaban 5 mg BID (or 2.5 mg BID if ≥ 2 of: age ≥ 80, weight ≤ 60 kg, creatinine ≥ 133 µmol/L)
  - Rivaroxaban 20 mg OD with evening meal
  - Dabigatran 150 mg BID (110 mg BID if age ≥ 80 or interacting drug)
- **VKA (warfarin):** Only when NOAC contraindicated; target INR 2–3 with TTR > 70%
- **Contraindication:** Antiplatelet therapy alone is NOT recommended for stroke prevention in AF

## Bleeding Risk — HAS-BLED Score

| Factor | Score |
|---|---|
| H — Hypertension (uncontrolled, SBP > 160) | 1 |
| A — Abnormal renal/liver function | 1–2 |
| S — Stroke history | 1 |
| B — Bleeding history or predisposition | 1 |
| L — Labile INR (TTR < 60%, if on VKA) | 1 |
| E — Elderly (age > 65) | 1 |
| D — Drugs (antiplatelets, NSAIDs) or alcohol | 1–2 |

HAS-BLED ≥ 3 = high risk; use to identify and correct modifiable bleeding risk factors, not to withhold OAC.

## Rate Control

- **Target:** Resting HR < 110 bpm (lenient); < 80 bpm if symptomatic
- **First-line:** Beta-blockers (metoprolol, bisoprolol) or non-DHP CCBs (verapamil, diltiazem)
- **Add-on:** Digoxin (especially in HFrEF, sedentary patients)
- **Avoid verapamil/diltiazem** in HFrEF (EF < 40%)

## Rhythm Control

- Preferred in symptomatic AF, younger patients, first-diagnosed AF, HF with AF
- **Cardioversion:** Electrical (DCCV) or pharmacological (flecainide, propafenone if no structural disease; amiodarone universally)
- **Antiarrhythmic drugs (AAD):** Flecainide/propafenone (no structural heart disease), sotalol, amiodarone, dronedarone
- **Catheter ablation:** Pulmonary vein isolation (PVI) — first-line option in paroxysmal/persistent AF if AAD failed or patient preference

## Integrated AF Care — ABC Pathway

- **A:** Anticoagulation / Avoid stroke
- **B:** Better symptom management (rate/rhythm control)
- **C:** Cardiovascular risk factor and comorbidity management

Cookies may be set by the website you are visiting (‘first party cookies’) or they may be set by other websites who run content on the page you are viewing (‘third party cookies’). When you use our website, services, applications, messaging and tools, the ESC or authorised service providers may collect information to operate and improve your experience on our websites. If you don’t want to receive any cookies, you can modify your browser settings so that it notifies you when cookies are sent to it or you can refuse cookies altogether. You can also delete cookies that have already been set. The ESC also offers you the possibility to opt-out of each cookie category (except strictly necessary cookies) by clicking through the various tabs on the left. 

[Read our full Cookies Policy](https://www.escardio.org/The-ESC/About/Policies/cookies)

#### Strictly Necessary Cookies

Always Active

These cookies are necessary for the website to function and cannot be switched off in our systems. They are usually only set in response to actions made by you which amount to a request for services, such as setting your privacy preferences, logging in or filling in forms. You can set your browser to block or alert you about these cookies, but some parts of the site will not then work. These cookies do not store any personally identifiable information.
