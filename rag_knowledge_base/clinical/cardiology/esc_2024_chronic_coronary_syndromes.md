# ESC 2024 Guidelines on Chronic Coronary Syndromes (CCS)

## Definition

Chronic Coronary Syndromes (formerly stable CAD) encompass stable angina, asymptomatic CAD, post-ACS stabilisation, ischaemic cardiomyopathy, and vasospastic/microvascular angina.

## Diagnosis

### Symptom Assessment
- **Typical angina:** (1) substernal chest discomfort, (2) provoked by exertion/stress, (3) relieved by rest or nitrate within 5 min
- Atypical angina: 2 of 3 features
- Non-cardiac: 0–1 features

### Pre-test Probability of Obstructive CAD
- Use age, sex, symptom type, risk factors
- High PTP (≥ 15%): proceed to functional imaging or CTCA
- Low PTP (< 5%): other diagnoses more likely

### Non-Invasive Testing

| Test | Indication |
|---|---|
| CT Coronary Angiography (CTCA) | First-line for intermediate PTP, good sensitivity |
| Stress Echo | Known or suspected LV dysfunction, post-revascularisation |
| Stress MRI | High diagnostic accuracy; preferred at specialist centres |
| Myocardial perfusion scintigraphy | Where CTCA non-diagnostic |
| Exercise ECG | Only if imaging not available; low sensitivity |

## Medical Management (OMT — Optimal Medical Therapy)

### Anti-Anginal Therapy
1. **Beta-blockers** (first-line): reduce HR, myocardial O₂ demand
2. **Long-acting nitrates** (isosorbide mononitrate): ensure nitrate-free interval ≥ 8h
3. **CCB:** Amlodipine (vasodilator) or diltiazem/verapamil (rate-limiting); useful if beta-blocker intolerant
4. **Ranolazine:** Add-on; late sodium channel inhibitor
5. **Ivabradine:** If beta-blocker contraindicated, sinus rhythm, HR > 70 bpm, EF ≥ 35%
6. **Nicorandil, trimetazidine:** 2nd-line metabolic agents

### Secondary Prevention
- **Antiplatelet:** Aspirin 75–100 mg daily indefinitely
  - Clopidogrel 75 mg if aspirin intolerant
  - Dual antiplatelet (DAPT): ASA + P2Y12 for 12 months post-ACS
- **Statins:** High-intensity (atorvastatin 40–80 mg or rosuvastatin 20–40 mg); target LDL-C < 1.4 mmol/L and > 50% reduction
- **ACEi/ARB:** Indicated if HTN, DM, HFrEF, or LV dysfunction
- **Beta-blocker:** Indicated post-MI, HFrEF

## Revascularisation

- **PCI vs CABG — decision by Heart Team:**
  - 3-vessel CAD + diabetes: CABG preferred (FREEDOM trial)
  - Left main disease (SYNTAX score ≤ 22): CABG or PCI equivalent (EXCEL/NOBLE)
  - Left main disease (SYNTAX score > 32): CABG preferred
  - Single/2-vessel CAD: PCI preferred

- **OMT vs Revascularisation (ISCHEMIA trial):** No mortality difference in stable CCS without left main disease — revascularise for symptom control or proximal LAD/left main

## Refractory Angina

- When symptoms persist despite OMT and revascularisation not feasible
- Enhanced external counterpulsation (EECP)
- Spinal cord stimulation
- Transmyocardial laser revascularisation

## Priority Level

- Stable angina with good response to medication: **ROUTINE**
- Uncontrolled angina, new ECG changes: **PRIORITY**
- Suspected ACS (rest pain, troponin rise): **URGENT — treat as ACS**

# 2024 ESC Guidelines for the management of chronic coronary syndromes

 30 Aug, 2024 

1.   [Home](https://www.escardio.org/)
2.   [Guidelines](https://www.escardio.org/guidelines/)
3.   [ESC Clinical Practice Guidelines](https://www.escardio.org/guidelines/clinical-practice-guidelines/)
4.   [All ESC Clinical Practice Guidelines](https://www.escardio.org/guidelines/clinical-practice-guidelines/all-esc-practice-guidelines/)
5.   2024 Chronic Coronary Syndromes

 The current guidelines for managing chronic coronary syndromes (CCS) are intended to assist in diagnosing and initiating treatment for individuals with suspected CCS, as well as in the longterm management of patients with confirmed CCS. 

These guidelines recommend using a risk factor-weighted clinical likelihood model to estimate the most up-to-date clinical likelihood of obstructive coronary artery disease (CAD). Compared to the basic 2019 ESC pre-test probability model, incorporating risk factors into the pre-test likelihood model (which considers age, sex, and symptoms) enhances the prediction of obstructive CAD. This approach reclassifies more individuals into the very low and low likelihood categories, where further testing may be unnecessary. An updated diagnostic algorithm is provided for patients with moderate or high pre-test likelihood. The Guidelines also include an expanded section on diagnosing and treating patients with angina and no or nonobstructive coronary artery disease, now termed ANOCA-INOCA. The treatment section offers guidance on anti-anginal/anti-ischaemic medications and various event-preventing therapies. It also covers strategies to improve adherence to medical treatment and prescribed lifestyle changes. Additionally, the guidelines review indications for and selection of the optimal revascularisation modality based on findings from large RCTs and IPDAs. Lastly, options for managing recurrent or refractory angina/ischaemia are discussed.

#### Your Privacy

Cookies may be set by the website you are visiting (‘first party cookies’) or they may be set by other websites who run content on the page you are viewing (‘third party cookies’). When you use our website, services, applications, messaging and tools, the ESC or authorised service providers may collect information to operate and improve your experience on our websites. If you don’t want to receive any cookies, you can modify your browser settings so that it notifies you when cookies are sent to it or you can refuse cookies altogether. You can also delete cookies that have already been set. The ESC also offers you the possibility to opt-out of each cookie category (except strictly necessary cookies) by clicking through the various tabs on the left. 

[Read our full Cookies Policy](https://www.escardio.org/The-ESC/About/Policies/cookies)
