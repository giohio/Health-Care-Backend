# Vascular Lesions of the Skin

## Classification

### Vascular Tumours (Proliferative)

| Lesion | Key Features | Management |
|---|---|---|
| Infantile haemangioma | Bright red, appears 1–4 weeks; involutes by age 5–10 | Propranolol if vision/airway at risk; reassure otherwise |
| Congenital haemangioma (RICH/NICH) | Present at birth; RICH involutes, NICH does not | Observation; surgery for NICH if cosmetic |
| Pyogenic granuloma | Rapidly growing, friable, bleeds easily; post-trauma/pregnancy | Shave excision + cautery; curettage |
| Angiosarcoma | Ill-defined violaceous patch/plaque, elderly face/scalp; post-radiation | Surgery + radiotherapy; poor prognosis |
| Kaposi sarcoma | Violaceous patches/nodules; HHV-8; HIV or immunosuppressed | HAART for HIV-associated; radiotherapy for localised |

### Vascular Malformations (Non-Proliferative, Present at Birth)

| Type | Clinical Feature | Management |
|---|---|---|
| Port-wine stain (capillary) | Flat, macular, pink–purple; face; may darken with age | Pulsed dye laser (PDL); may indicate Sturge-Weber syndrome |
| Venous malformation | Soft, compressible, bluish; phleboliths on imaging | Sclerotherapy, surgery |
| Arteriovenous malformation (AVM) | Warm, pulsatile mass; thrill/bruit; risk of bleeding | Embolisation ± surgery |
| Lymphatic malformation (cystic hygroma) | Transilluminates; neck/axilla in children | Surgery or sclerotherapy |

### Benign Vascular Lesions

| Lesion | Features | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Spider angioma (naevus araneus) | Central arteriole + radiating capillaries; blanches on pressure | Associated with liver disease, pregnancy, high oestrogen; > 5 = investigate liver |
| Cherry angioma (Campbell de Morgan spot) | Bright red dome-shaped papule; adults > 30; trunk | Benign; treat only if cosmetic |
| Angiokeratoma | Dark red/black, hyperkeratotic papule; scrotum (Fordyce), digits | Angiokeratoma corporis diffusum = Fabry disease (alpha-galactosidase A deficiency) |
| Telangiectasia | Dilated superficial capillaries; face/legs | Primary (rosacea, CREST) or secondary (sun damage, radiation) |
| Glomus tumour | Small, painful, subungual; triad: pinpoint pain, cold sensitivity, pressure tenderness | MRI; surgical excision |

## CREST Syndrome and Telangiectasia

- Calcinosis, Raynaud’s, Esophageal dysmotility, Sclerodactyly, Telangiectasia
- Anti-centromere antibody positive
- Risk of pulmonary arterial hypertension

## Hereditary Haemorrhagic Telangiectasia (HHT / Osler-Weber-Rendu)

- AD inheritance, ENG or ACVRL1 mutation
- Criteria (Curaçao): recurrent epistaxis, mucocutaneous telangiectasias, visceral AVMs (lungs, liver, brain), first-degree relative with HHT
- Diagnose if ≥ 3 criteria; probable if 2
- Screen for pulmonary AVM with contrast echo

## Dermoscopy of Vascular Lesions

| Pattern | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| Red/red-blue lacunae | Haemangioma |
| Red homogeneous area | Pyogenic granuloma |
| Dotted/linear vessels on white background | Dermoscopic of amelanotic melanoma — exclude! |

## Priority Level

- Benign cherry angioma, spider angioma: **ROUTINE**
- Rapidly growing vascular tumour, angiosarcoma suspicion: **PRIORITY**
- Bleeding AVM, gangrenous Kaposi, acute haemorrhage: **URGENT**
