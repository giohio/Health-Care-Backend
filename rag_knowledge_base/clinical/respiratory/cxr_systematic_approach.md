# Chest X-Ray (CXR) — Systematic Interpretation Approach

## Quality Check First (RIPE)

- **R**otation: Medial ends of clavicles equidistant from spinous process of T4
- **I**nspiration: 5–6 anterior ribs or 8–10 posterior ribs visible above diaphragm
- **P**enetration: Lower thoracic vertebral bodies just visible through cardiac shadow
- **E**xposure: Adequate density to assess lung fields

## Systematic Review — ABCDE

### A — Airways
- Trachea: midline? Deviation away from = tension pneumothorax, large effusion; toward = collapse/fibrosis
- Carina angle < 70° (splaying suggests left atrial enlargement)
- Check for foreign body, endotracheal tube position (2–3 cm above carina)

### B — Breathing / Lung Fields

| Finding | Description | Common Causes |
|---|---|---|
| Consolidation | Airspace opacity, air bronchogram visible | Pneumonia, aspiration, haemorrhage |
| Collapse/Atelectasis | Opacity with volume loss, shift of fissures | Mucus plug, endobronchial lesion |
| Pleural effusion | Blunting of costophrenic angle; meniscus sign | Heart failure, empyema, malignancy, hypoalbuminaemia |
| Pneumothorax | Visceral pleural line, absent lung markings | Trauma, COPD, tall thin male (primary) |
| Interstitial pattern | Reticular or reticulonodular shadowing | Pulmonary oedema, fibrosis, infection |
| Nodule/Mass | Round opacity; solitary vs multiple | Cancer, granuloma, abscess, AVM |
| Hyperinflation | Flattened diaphragm, > 6 anterior ribs | COPD, severe asthma |

### C — Cardiac
- Cardiothoracic ratio (CTR): heart width / chest width < 0.5 (on PA erect)
  - CTR > 0.5 = cardiomegaly (LVH, dilated cardiomyopathy, pericardial effusion)
- Right heart border: right atrium; left heart border: LV (lower), LA auricle (middle), aortic knuckle (upper)
- Double right heart border = left atrial enlargement
- Boot-shaped heart = tetralogy of Fallot

### D — Diaphragm
- Right hemidiaphragm normally 1.5–2.5 cm higher than left (due to liver)
- Both visible in pneumoperitoneum (free air under diaphragm)
- Raised left hemidiaphragm: phrenic nerve palsy, subphrenic abscess, ruptured spleen
- Blunt angle costophrenic = effusion (> 200–300 mL)

### E — Everything Else (Extrathoracic)
- Bones: rib fractures, lytic lesions, rib notching (coarctation)
- Soft tissues: surgical emphysema, mastectomy, pacemaker leads
- Lines/tubes: ETT, CVP, NG tube position, chest drains
- Abdomen: bowel gas pattern, calcifications

## Common Patterns and Diagnoses

| Pattern | Key Feature | Think |
|---|---|---|
| Bat-wing / butterfly opacities | Perihilar bilateral | Pulmonary oedema |
| Air bronchograms | Air in bronchi within opacity | Consolidation (not effusion/collapse) |
| Silhouette sign | Loss of normal border | Adjacent pathology (e.g., RML consolidation → right heart border lost) |
| Kerley B lines | Short horizontal lines at lung bases | Pulmonary oedema (interstitial) |
| Cavitation within consolidation | Thick-walled cavity | TB, lung abscess, Wegener’s |

## Priority Level

- Incidental finding, stable patient: **ROUTINE**
- New consolidation, effusion requiring intervention: **PRIORITY**
- Tension pneumothorax, massive effusion, acute pulmonary oedema: **URGENT**
