**Pulmonology** (, , from [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin")_pulmō, -ōnis_ "lung" and the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek "Ancient Greek") suffix [-λογία](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/-%CE%BB%CE%BF%CE%B3%CE%AF%CE%B1 "wikt:-λογία")_-logía_ "study of"), **pneumology** (, built on Greek πνεύμων _pneúmōn_ "lung") or **pneumonology**[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-1) () is a [medical specialty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specialty_(medicine) "Specialty (medicine)") that deals with [diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_disease "Respiratory disease") involving the [respiratory tract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_tract "Respiratory tract").[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-acp-2) It is also known as **respirology**, **respiratory medicine**, or **chest medicine** in some countries and areas.

Pulmonology is considered a branch of [internal medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_medicine "Internal medicine"), and is related to [intensive care medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intensive_care_medicine "Intensive care medicine"). Pulmonology often involves managing patients who need life support and [mechanical ventilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_ventilation "Mechanical ventilation"). Pulmonologists are specially trained in diseases and conditions of the [chest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chest "Chest"), particularly [pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumonia "Pneumonia"), [asthma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asthma "Asthma"), [tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis "Tuberculosis"), [emphysema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emphysema "Emphysema"), and complicated chest infections.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-3)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-4)

Pulmonology/respirology departments work especially closely with certain other specialties: [cardiothoracic surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiothoracic_surgery "Cardiothoracic surgery") departments and [cardiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiology "Cardiology") departments.

## History of pulmonology

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pulmonology&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: History of pulmonology")]

One of the first major discoveries relevant to the field of pulmonology was the discovery of [pulmonary circulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_circulation "Pulmonary circulation"). Originally, it was thought that [blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood "Blood") reaching the right side of the [heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart "Heart") passed through small 'pores' in the [septum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interventricular_septum "Interventricular septum") into the left side to be oxygenated, as theorized by [Galen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galen "Galen"); however, the discovery of pulmonary circulation disproves this theory, which had previously been accepted since the 2nd century. Thirteenth-century [anatomist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomist "Anatomist") and [physiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiologist "Physiologist")[Ibn Al-Nafis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Al-Nafis "Ibn Al-Nafis") accurately theorized that there was no 'direct' passage between the two sides ([ventricles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventricle_(heart) "Ventricle (heart)")) of the heart. He believed that the blood must have passed through the [pulmonary artery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_artery "Pulmonary artery"), through the lungs, and back into the heart to be [pumped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circulatory_system "Circulatory system") around the body. This is believed by many to be the first scientific description of pulmonary circulation.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-5)

Although pulmonary medicine only began to evolve as a medical specialty in the 1950s, [William Welch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_H._Welch "William H. Welch") and [William Osler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Osler "William Osler") founded the 'parent' organization of the [American Thoracic Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Thoracic_Society "American Thoracic Society"),[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-6) the National Association for the Study and Prevention of [Tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis "Tuberculosis").[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-7) The care, treatment, and study of tuberculosis of the lung is recognised as a discipline in its own right, [phthisiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phthisiology "Phthisiology").[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-8) When the specialty began to evolve, several discoveries were being made linking the respiratory system and the measurement of arterial blood gases, attracting more and more physicians and researchers to the developing field.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-9)

## Pulmonology and its relevance in other medical fields

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pulmonology&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Pulmonology and its relevance in other medical fields")]

[Surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surgery "Surgery") of the respiratory tract is generally performed by specialists in [cardiothoracic surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiothoracic_surgery "Cardiothoracic surgery")[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-10) (or [thoracic surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoracic_surgery "Thoracic surgery")),[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-11) though minor procedures may be performed by pulmonologists. Pulmonology is closely related to critical care medicine[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-12) when dealing with patients who require mechanical ventilation. As a result, many pulmonologists are certified to practice critical care medicine in addition to pulmonary medicine. There are fellowship programs that allow physicians to become board certified in pulmonary and critical care medicine simultaneously. [Interventional pulmonology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interventional_pulmonology "Interventional pulmonology") is a relatively new field within pulmonary medicine[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-13) that deals with the use of procedures such as [bronchoscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronchoscopy "Bronchoscopy")[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-14) and pleuroscopy to treat several pulmonary diseases.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-15) Interventional pulmonology is increasingly recognized as a specific medical specialty.[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-16)

The pulmonologist begins the diagnostic process with a general review focusing on:

*   [hereditary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heredity "Heredity") diseases affecting the [lungs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_lung "Human lung") ([cystic fibrosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cystic_fibrosis "Cystic fibrosis"),[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-17)[alpha 1-antitrypsin deficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_1-antitrypsin_deficiency "Alpha 1-antitrypsin deficiency"))
*   exposure to [toxicants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toxicant "Toxicant") ([tobacco smoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_smoking "Tobacco smoking"),[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-BrelandSoule2017-18)[asbestos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asbestos "Asbestos"), [exhaust fumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exhaust_gas "Exhaust gas"), [coal mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_mining "Coal mining") fumes, [e-cigarette aerosol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composition_of_electronic_cigarette_aerosol "Composition of electronic cigarette aerosol")[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-PerikleousSteiropoulos2018-19))
*   exposure to [infectious agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_disease "Infectious disease") (certain types of [birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird "Bird"), malt processing)
*   an [autoimmune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoimmunity "Autoimmunity")[diathesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diathesis_(medicine) "Diathesis (medicine)") that might predispose to certain conditions ([pulmonary fibrosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_fibrosis "Pulmonary fibrosis"), [pulmonary hypertension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_hypertension "Pulmonary hypertension"))

Physical diagnostics[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-20) are as important as in other fields of [medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine "Medicine").

*   Inspection of the hands for signs of [cyanosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanosis "Cyanosis") or [clubbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nail_clubbing "Nail clubbing"), the chest wall, and the respiratory rate.
*   Palpation of the [cervical lymph nodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cervical_lymph_nodes "Cervical lymph nodes"), [trachea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebrate_trachea "Vertebrate trachea") and chest wall movement.
*   [Percussion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percussion_(medicine) "Percussion (medicine)") of the lung fields for dullness or hyper-resonance.
*   [Auscultation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auscultation "Auscultation") (with a [stethoscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stethoscope "Stethoscope")) of the lung fields for diminished or unusual [breath sounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breath_sounds "Breath sounds"). 
    *   [Rales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rales "Rales") or [rhonchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhonchi "Rhonchi") are heard over the lung fields with a [stethoscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stethoscope "Stethoscope").

As many [heart diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiology "Cardiology") can give pulmonary signs,[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-21) a thorough cardiac investigation is usually included.

### Clinical procedures

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pulmonology&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Clinical procedures")]

Pulmonary clinical procedures include the following pulmonary tests and procedures:[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-22)[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-23)

*   [Medical laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_laboratory "Medical laboratory") investigation of blood ([blood tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_test "Blood test")). Sometimes [arterial blood gas tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arterial_blood_gas_test "Arterial blood gas test") are also required.
*   [Spirometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirometry "Spirometry") is the determination of maximum airflow at a given lung volume as measured by breathing into a dedicated machine; this is the key test to diagnose airflow obstruction.
*   [Pulmonary function testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_function_testing "Pulmonary function testing") including spirometry, as above, plus response to bronchodilators, lung volumes, and diffusion capacity, the latter being a measure of lung oxygen absorptive area
*   [Bronchoscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronchoscopy "Bronchoscopy")[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-24) with [bronchoalveolar lavage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronchoalveolar_lavage "Bronchoalveolar lavage") (BAL), endobronchial and transbronchial [biopsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biopsy "Biopsy") and [epithelial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epithelium "Epithelium") brushing
*   [Chest X-rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chest_radiograph "Chest radiograph")[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-25)
*   [CT scan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CT_scan "CT scan")[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-26)
*   [Scintigraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scintigraphy "Scintigraphy") and other methods of [nuclear medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_medicine "Nuclear medicine")
*   [Positron emission tomography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positron_emission_tomography "Positron emission tomography")[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-27) (especially in [lung cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung_cancer "Lung cancer"))
*   [Polysomnography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polysomnography "Polysomnography") (sleep studies[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-28)) is commonly used for the diagnosis of [sleep apnea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleep_apnea "Sleep apnea")

### Surgical procedures

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pulmonology&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Surgical procedures")]

Major surgical procedures on the heart and lungs are performed by a [thoracic surgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoracic_surgery "Thoracic surgery").[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-29) Pulmonologists often perform specialized procedures to get samples from the inside of the chest or the inside of the lung. They use radiographic techniques to view the vasculature of the lungs and heart to assist with diagnosis.

## Treatment and therapeutics

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pulmonology&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Treatment and therapeutics")]

[Medication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medication "Medication") is the most important treatment for most diseases of pulmonology, either by inhalation ([bronchodilators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronchodilator "Bronchodilator") and [steroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steroid "Steroid")) or in oral form ([antibiotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibiotic "Antibiotic"), [leukotriene antagonists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukotriene_antagonist "Leukotriene antagonist")). A common example being the usage of [inhalers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhalers "Inhalers") in the treatment of inflammatory lung conditions such as [asthma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asthma "Asthma") or [chronic obstructive pulmonary disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronic_obstructive_pulmonary_disease "Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease"). [Oxygen therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen_therapy "Oxygen therapy")[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-30) is often necessary in severe respiratory disease ([emphysema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emphysema "Emphysema") and [pulmonary fibrosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_fibrosis "Pulmonary fibrosis")). When this is insufficient, the patient might require [mechanical ventilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_ventilation "Mechanical ventilation").

Pulmonary rehabilitation[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-31) has been defined as a multidimensional continuum of services directed to persons with pulmonary disease and their families, usually by an interdisciplinary team of specialists, with the goal of achieving and maintaining the individual's maximum level of independence and functioning in the community. Pulmonary rehabilitation[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-32) is intended to educate the patient, the family, and improve the overall quality of life and [prognosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prognosis "Prognosis") for the patient. Interventions can include exercise, education, emotional support, oxygen, [noninvasive mechanical ventilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-invasive_ventilation "Non-invasive ventilation"), optimization of airway secretion clearance, promoting compliance with medical care to reduce numbers of exacerbations and hospitalizations, and returning to work and/or a more active and emotionally satisfying life. These goals are appropriate for any patients with diminished respiratory reserve whether due to obstructive or intrinsic pulmonary diseases (oxygenation impairment) or neuromuscular weakness (ventilatory impairment). A pulmonary rehabilitation team[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-33) may include a rehabilitation physician, a pulmonary medicine specialist, [physician assistant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physician_assistant "Physician assistant") and allied health professionals including a rehabilitation [nurse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurse "Nurse"), a [respiratory therapist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_therapist "Respiratory therapist"), a [speech-language pathologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech%E2%80%93language_pathology "Speech–language pathology"), a [physical therapist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_therapist "Physical therapist"), an [occupational therapist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupational_therapist "Occupational therapist"), a [psychologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychologist "Psychologist"), and a [social worker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_worker "Social worker") among others. Additionally, breathing games are used to motivate children to perform pulmonary rehabilitation.

In the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States"), pulmonologists are physicians who, after receiving a medical degree ([MD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Medicine "Doctor of Medicine") or [DO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Osteopathic_Medicine "Doctor of Osteopathic Medicine")), complete [residency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residency_(medicine) "Residency (medicine)") training in [internal medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_medicine "Internal medicine"), followed by at least two additional years of subspeciality [fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellowship_(medicine) "Fellowship (medicine)") training in pulmonology.[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-34) After satisfactorily completing a fellowship in pulmonary medicine, the physician is permitted to take the board certification examination in pulmonary medicine. After passing this exam, the physician is then board certified as a pulmonologist. Most pulmonologists complete three years of combined subspecialty fellowship training in pulmonary medicine and critical care medicine.

### Pediatric pulmonologist

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pulmonology&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Pediatric pulmonologist")]

In the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States"), pediatric pulmonologists[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonology#cite_note-35) are [physicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicians "Physicians") who, after receiving a medical degree (MD, DO, MBBS, MBBCh, etc.), complete residency training in [pediatrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pediatrics "Pediatrics"), followed by at least three additional years of subspeciality fellowship training in pulmonology. Pediatric pulmonologists treat diseases of the airways, lungs, respiratory mechanics and aerodigestive system.

## Scientific research

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pulmonology&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Scientific research")]

Pulmonologists are involved in both clinical and basic research of the [respiratory system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_system "Respiratory system"), ranging from the anatomy of the [respiratory epithelium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_epithelium "Respiratory epithelium") to the most effective treatment of [pulmonary hypertension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_hypertension "Pulmonary hypertension"). Scientific research also takes place to look for causes and possible treatment in diseases such as [pulmonary tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_tuberculosis "Pulmonary tuberculosis") and [lung cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung_cancer "Lung cancer").
