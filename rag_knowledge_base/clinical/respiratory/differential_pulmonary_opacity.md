A **pulmonary consolidation** is a region of normally compressible [lung tissue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parenchyma#In_animals "Parenchyma") that has been filled with liquid instead of air.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_consolidation#cite_note-1) The condition is marked by induration[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_consolidation#cite_note-2) (swelling or hardening of normally soft tissue) of a normally aerated lung. It is considered a [radiologic sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiologic_sign "Radiologic sign"). Consolidation occurs through accumulation of inflammatory cellular exudate in the [alveoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_alveolus "Pulmonary alveolus") and adjoining ducts. The liquid can be [pulmonary edema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_edema "Pulmonary edema"), inflammatory [exudate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exudate "Exudate"), [pus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pus "Pus"), inhaled water, or blood (from bronchial tree or [hemorrhage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemorrhage "Hemorrhage") from a [pulmonary artery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_artery "Pulmonary artery")). Consolidation must be present to diagnose [pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumonia "Pneumonia"): the signs of lobar pneumonia are characteristic and clinically referred to as consolidation.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_consolidation#cite_note-Metlay-3)

## Signs

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pulmonary_consolidation&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Signs")]

Signs that consolidation may have occurred include:

*   Expansion of the thorax on inspiration is reduced on the affected side
*   [Vocal fremitus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocal_fremitus "Vocal fremitus") is increased on the affected side
*   Percussion note is impaired in the affected area
*   Breath sounds are bronchial
*   Possible medium, late, or pan-inspiratory [crackles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crackles "Crackles")
*   [Vocal resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocal_resonance "Vocal resonance") is increased. Here, the patient's voice (or whisper, as in [whispered pectoriloquy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whispered_pectoriloquy "Whispered pectoriloquy")) can be heard more clearly when there is consolidation, as opposed to the healthy lung where speech sounds muffled.
*   A [pleural rub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleural_friction_rub "Pleural friction rub") may be present.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_consolidation#cite_note-talley-4)
*   A lower P A O 2 than calculated in the [alveolar gas equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alveolar_gas_equation "Alveolar gas equation")

## Diagnosis

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pulmonary_consolidation&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Diagnosis")]

### Radiology

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pulmonary_consolidation&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Radiology")]

*   Typically, an area of white lung is seen on a standard X-ray.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_consolidation#cite_note-5) Consolidated tissue is more radio-opaque than normally aerated lung parenchyma, so that it is clearly demonstrable in [radiography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiography "Radiography") and on [CT scans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_computed_tomography "X-ray computed tomography"). Consolidation is often a middle-to-late stage feature/complication in [pulmonary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary "Pulmonary") infections.
