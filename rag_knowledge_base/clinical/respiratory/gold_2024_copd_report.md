# GOLD 2024 — Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)

## Definition

COPD is a heterogeneous lung condition characterised by chronic respiratory symptoms (dyspnoea, cough, sputum, exacerbations) due to airway and/or alveolar abnormalities causing persistent, often progressive airflow obstruction confirmed by post-bronchodilator FEV₁/FVC < 0.70.

## Spirometric Severity (GOLD 1–4)

| Grade | FEV₁ (% predicted) | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| GOLD 1 | ≥ 80% | Mild |
| GOLD 2 | 50–79% | Moderate |
| GOLD 3 | 30–49% | Severe |
| GOLD 4 | < 30% | Very Severe |

*Spirometry required for diagnosis. Post-BD FEV₁/FVC < 0.70 confirms obstruction.*

## GOLD ABCD Assessment Tool (2024 Refined)

Based on **symptoms** (mMRC dyspnoea scale or CAT score) and **exacerbation history:**

| Group | Exacerbations | Symptoms |
|---|---|---|
| A | 0–1 (not leading to hospitalisation) | Low (mMRC 0–1 or CAT < 10) |
| B | 0–1 (not leading to hospitalisation) | High (mMRC ≥ 2 or CAT ≥ 10) |
| E | ≥ 2 or ≥ 1 leading to hospitalisation | Any |

*(Groups C and D merged into Group E in GOLD 2023+)*

## Pharmacological Management

### Initial Treatment

| Group | Initial Therapy |
|---|---|
| A | Short-acting bronchodilator (SABA or SAMA) PRN |
| B | Long-acting bronchodilator (LABA or LAMA) — prefer LAMA |
| E | LAMA + LABA (dual bronchodilation); consider triple if blood eos ≥ 300 cells/µL |

### Escalation
- If symptoms persist on LAMA: add LABA → LAMA + LABA
- If exacerbations on LAMA + LABA with eos ≥ 100 cells/µL: add ICS → triple therapy (LAMA + LABA + ICS)
- **ICS indications:** frequent exacerbations + eos ≥ 300, or eos ≥ 100 with ≥ 2 exacerbations/year
- **Avoid ICS alone** in COPD (increased pneumonia risk without LABA)

### Key Drugs

| Class | Examples |
|---|---|
| SABA | Salbutamol (albuterol), levalbuterol |
| SAMA | Ipratropium |
| LABA | Formoterol, salmeterol, indacaterol, olodaterol |
| LAMA | Tiotropium, glycopyrronium, umeclidinium, aclidinium |
| ICS | Budesonide, fluticasone, beclomethasone |
| Triple (single inhaler) | FF/UMEC/VI (Trelegy), BDP/FF/GB (Trimbow) |

## Non-Pharmacological Management

- **Smoking cessation** (most important intervention — slows FEV₁ decline)
- **Pulmonary rehabilitation:** 8–12 weeks; improves dyspnoea, exercise capacity, QoL
- **Vaccination:** Influenza annually; pneumococcal (PCV15/PPSV23); COVID-19; Tdap
- **Oxygen therapy (LTOT):** if PaO₂ ≤ 55 mmHg or SpO₂ ≤ 88% at rest → ≥ 15h/day
- **NIV (BiPAP):** Acute hypercapnic respiratory failure; stable hypercapnia (PaCO₂ > 52 mmHg) → reduces hospitalisation

## Acute Exacerbation of COPD (AECOPD)

### Classification
- **Mild:** Managed with increased bronchodilators
- **Moderate:** Requires corticosteroids ± antibiotics (outpatient)
- **Severe:** Hospitalisation required
- **Life-threatening:** ICU, mechanical ventilation

### Management
1. Bronchodilators: SABA ± SAMA (nebulised)
2. Systemic corticosteroids: prednisolone 40 mg × 5 days
3. Antibiotics: if purulent sputum, increased dyspnoea + increased sputum + purulence (Anthonisen criteria)
   - Amoxicillin, doxycycline, or azithromycin; co-amoxiclav if severe
4. Controlled oxygen: target SpO₂ 88–92% (not 95–100% — hypercapnia risk)
5. NIV if pH < 7.35 and PaCO₂ > 45 mmHg
6. Thromboprophylaxis (LMWH)

## Priority Level

- Stable COPD on maintenance therapy: **ROUTINE**
- Moderate exacerbation: **PRIORITY**
- Severe exacerbation (SpO₂ < 88%, pH < 7.35): **URGENT**
