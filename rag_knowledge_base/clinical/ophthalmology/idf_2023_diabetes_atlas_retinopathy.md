# Diabetic Retinopathy — IDF/AAO Guidelines

## Definition

Diabetic retinopathy (DR) is a microvascular complication of diabetes mellitus affecting the retinal vasculature. Leading cause of preventable blindness in working-age adults worldwide.

## Epidemiology (IDF Diabetes Atlas 2023)

- ~537 million adults with diabetes worldwide
- ~35% of all diabetics have some degree of DR
- ~10% have vision-threatening DR (VTDR = PDR or CSME)
- Risk increases with duration of diabetes and poor glycaemic control

## Classification (ETDRS / ICDR)

### Non-Proliferative Diabetic Retinopathy (NPDR)

| Stage | Key Features |
|---|---|
| Mild NPDR | Microaneurysms only |
| Moderate NPDR | Microaneurysms + dot/blot haemorrhages, hard exudates, cotton wool spots |
| Severe NPDR (4-2-1 rule) | > 20 haemorrhages in 4 quadrants; OR venous beading in ≥ 2 quadrants; OR IRMA in ≥ 1 quadrant |
| Very Severe NPDR | 2 or more of 4-2-1 features |

### Proliferative Diabetic Retinopathy (PDR)

| Stage | Feature |
|---|---|
| Early PDR | New vessels on disc (NVD) or elsewhere (NVE) |
| High-risk PDR | NVD ≥ 1/4–1/3 disc area; NVD with vitreous/preretinal haemorrhage; NVE + haemorrhage |
| Advanced PDR | Traction retinal detachment, rubeosis iridis (neovascularisation of iris) |

### Diabetic Macular Oedema (DME)

- **Centre-involving DME (CI-DME):** Oedema at or within 1 mm of foveal centre → greatest threat to visual acuity
- Diagnosed by OCT (spectral domain preferred): central retinal thickness > 250–300 µm

## Screening Guidelines

| Diabetes Type | First Screen | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Type 1 DM | 5 years after diagnosis (or at puberty) | Annually if no DR; 6-monthly if mild/moderate NPDR |
| Type 2 DM | At time of diagnosis | Annually if no DR; 3–6 monthly if NPDR |
| Gestational DM | First trimester if pre-existing DM | Every 1–3 months during pregnancy |

**Screening modality:** Digital fundus photography (mydriatic or non-mydriatic); 7-field ETDRS standard; telemedicine screening acceptable.

## Risk Factors for Progression

- Duration of diabetes (strongest risk factor)
- Poor glycaemic control (HbA1c > 7%)
- Hypertension (↑ by 35% if uncontrolled)
- Dyslipidaemia (promotes hard exudates)
- Pregnancy (rapid DR progression in T1DM)
- Renal disease/proteinuria
- Smoking

## Treatment

### Medical / Systemic
- Optimise HbA1c (target < 7% / 53 mmol/mol) — DCCT/UKPDS
- Blood pressure control (target < 130/80 mmHg)
- Lipid control (statin; fenofibrate reduces DR progression in T2DM — FIELD trial)

### Intravitreal Injections
- **Anti-VEGF (1st line for CI-DME and PDR):** Ranibizumab, bevacizumab, aflibercept, faricimab
  - Faricimab (Ang-2 + VEGF-A): longer durability, every 4 months after loading
  - Treat-and-extend or PRN protocols used after initial monthly loading
- **Intravitreal corticosteroids:** Dexamethasone implant (Ozurdex) for DME — pseudophakic eyes; short-term effect

### Laser Photocoagulation
- **Panretinal photocoagulation (PRP):** For high-risk PDR; reduces neovascularisation risk 50%
  - Partially replaced by anti-VEGF (DRCR.net Protocol S)
- **Focal/grid laser:** For non-centre-involving DME (centre-sparing approach)
  - Largely replaced by anti-VEGF for CI-DME

### Vitreoretinal Surgery
- For: non-clearing vitreous haemorrhage, traction retinal detachment involving macula, combined traction/rhegmatogenous RD
- Pars plana vitrectomy (PPV) with membrane peeling ± PRP ± silicone oil tamponade

## Grading for Clinical Urgency

| Finding | Action |
|---|---|
| No DR, mild NPDR | Annual monitoring |
| Moderate NPDR | 6-monthly monitoring; optimise risk factors |
| Severe NPDR | 3-monthly; refer retina specialist |
| PDR without high-risk features | Anti-VEGF or PRP; close follow-up |
| High-risk PDR | **Urgent** anti-VEGF + PRP; vitrectomy if indicated |
| CI-DME with vision loss | Anti-VEGF; monthly until stable |
| Vitreous haemorrhage, TRD | **URGENT** vitreoretinal surgery referral |

## Priority Level

- No DR or mild NPDR, stable: **ROUTINE**
- Moderate–severe NPDR, new CI-DME: **PRIORITY**
- High-risk PDR, vitreous haemorrhage, TRD: **URGENT**
