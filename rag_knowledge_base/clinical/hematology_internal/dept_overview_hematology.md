**Hematology** ([spelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_and_British_English_spelling_differences#ae_and_oe "American and British English spelling differences")**haematology** in [British English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English "British English")) is the branch of [medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine "Medicine") concerned with the study of the cause, prognosis, treatment, and prevention of diseases related to [blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood "Blood").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hematology#cite_note-1)[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hematology#cite_note-News-Medical.net-2009-2) It involves treating diseases that affect the production of blood and its components, such as [blood cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_cells "Blood cells"), [hemoglobin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemoglobin "Hemoglobin"), [blood proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_proteins "Blood proteins"), [bone marrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone_marrow "Bone marrow"), [platelets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platelet "Platelet"), [blood vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_vessel "Blood vessel"), [spleen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spleen "Spleen"), and the mechanism of [coagulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coagulation "Coagulation"). Such diseases might include [hemophilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemophilia "Hemophilia"), [sickle cell anemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sickle_cell_anemia "Sickle cell anemia"), blood clots ([thrombus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrombus "Thrombus")), other bleeding disorders, and blood [cancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer "Cancer") such as [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia "Leukemia"), [multiple myeloma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_myeloma "Multiple myeloma"), and [lymphoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphoma "Lymphoma").[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hematology#cite_note-AMA-3) The laboratory analysis of blood is frequently performed by a [medical technologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_technologist "Medical technologist") or [medical laboratory scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_laboratory_scientist "Medical laboratory scientist").

## Specialization

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Hematology&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Specialization")]

Physicians specialized in hematology are known as **hematologists** or **haematologists**.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hematology#cite_note-News-Medical.net-2009-2) Their routine work mainly includes the care and treatment of patients with hematological diseases, although some may also work at the hematology laboratory viewing [blood films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_film "Blood film") and [bone marrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone_marrow "Bone marrow") slides under the [microscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microscope "Microscope"), interpreting various hematological test results and [blood clotting test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_clotting_tests "Blood clotting tests") results. In some institutions, hematologists also manage the hematology laboratory.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hematology#cite_note-AMA-3) Physicians who work in hematology laboratories, and most commonly manage them, are pathologists specialized in the diagnosis of hematological diseases, referred to as **hematopathologists** or **haematopathologists**. Hematologists and hematopathologists generally work in conjunction to formulate a diagnosis and deliver the most appropriate therapy if needed. Hematology is a distinct subspecialty of internal medicine, separate from but overlapping with the subspecialty of medical [oncology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncology "Oncology"). Hematologists may specialize further or have special interests, for example, in:

*   treating bleeding disorders such as [hemophilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemophilia "Hemophilia") and [idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idiopathic_thrombocytopenic_purpura "Idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura"),[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hematology#cite_note-News-Medical.net-2009-2) with the latter of these two conditions being continuously studied by hematologists due to its unknown cause.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hematology#cite_note-4)
*   treating hematological malignancies such as [lymphoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphoma "Lymphoma") and [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia "Leukemia") (cancers)
*   treating [hemoglobinopathies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemoglobinopathies "Hemoglobinopathies"), including α-thalassemias and β-thalassemias (thalassemia syndromes) and hemoglobin S, hemoglobin C, and hemoglobin E (abnormal hemoglobins).[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hematology#cite_note-5)
*   the science of [blood transfusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_transfusion "Blood transfusion") and the work of a [blood bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_bank "Blood bank"), known as [transfusion medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfusion_medicine "Transfusion medicine")
*   bone marrow and [stem cell transplantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stem_cell_transplantation "Stem cell transplantation"), especially with the use of technologies to extract and isolate hematopoietic progenitor cells (HPCs).[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hematology#cite_note-6)

## Training

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Hematology&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Training")]

Starting hematologists (in the US) complete a four-year medical degree followed by three or four more years in residency or internship programs. After completion, they further expand their knowledge by spending two or three more years learning how to experiment, diagnose, and treat blood disorders.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hematology#cite_note-7) Some exposure to [hematopathology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hematopathology "Hematopathology") is typically included in their fellowship training. Job openings for hematologists require training in a recognized fellowship program to learn to diagnose and treat numerous blood-related benign conditions and [blood cancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_cancers "Blood cancers"). Hematologists typically work across specialties to care for patients with complex illnesses, such as [sickle cell disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sickle_cell_disease "Sickle cell disease"), who require complex, multidisciplinary care, and to provide consultation on cases of [disseminated intravascular coagulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disseminated_intravascular_coagulation "Disseminated intravascular coagulation"), [thrombosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrombosis "Thrombosis") and other conditions that can occur in hospitalized patients.[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hematology#cite_note-Systems-8)
