A **pituitary adenoma** is a [tumor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumor "Tumor") that occurs in the [pituitary gland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_gland "Pituitary gland"). Most pituitary tumors are benign, approximately 35% are invasive and just 0.1% to 0.2% are [carcinomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinoma "Carcinoma").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-nih-nci-1) Pituitary adenomas represent from 10% to 25% of all intracranial [neoplasms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoplasia "Neoplasia"), with an estimated [prevalence rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prevalence "Prevalence") in the general population of approximately 17%.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-nih-nci-1)[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-pmid15274075-2)

Non-invasive and non-secreting pituitary adenomas are considered to be [benign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benign_tumors "Benign tumors") in the literal as well as the clinical sense, though a 2011 [meta-analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta-analysis "Meta-analysis") of available research showed that research to either support or refute this assumption was scant and of questionable quality.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Balsells2011-3)

Adenomas exceeding 10 mm (0.39 in) in size are defined as _macroadenomas_, while those smaller than 10 mm (0.39 in) are referred to as _microadenomas_. Most pituitary adenomas are microadenomas and have an estimated prevalence of 16.7% (14.4% in [autopsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopsy "Autopsy") studies and 22.5% in [radiologic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_imaging "Medical imaging") studies).[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-pmid15274075-2)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-pmid18684022-4) The majority of pituitary microadenomas remain undiagnosed, and those that are diagnosed are often found as an [incidental finding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incidental_finding "Incidental finding") and are referred to as _[incidentalomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incidentaloma "Incidentaloma")_.

Pituitary macroadenomas are the most common cause of [hypopituitarism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypopituitarism "Hypopituitarism").[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-5)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-6)

While pituitary adenomas are common, affecting approximately 1 in 6 members of the general population, clinically active pituitary adenomas that require surgical treatment are more rare, affecting approximately 1 in 1,000.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-7)

## Signs and symptoms

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pituitary_adenoma&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Signs and symptoms")]

### Physical

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pituitary_adenoma&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Physical")]

Hormone secreting pituitary adenomas cause one of several forms of [hyperpituitarism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperpituitarism "Hyperpituitarism"). The specifics depend on the type of hormone. Some tumors secrete more than one hormone, the most common combination[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov-8) being [GH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_hormone "Growth hormone") and [prolactin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prolactin "Prolactin"), which present as [gigantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigantism "Gigantism") or [acromegaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acromegaly "Acromegaly") and [unexpected lactation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactorrhea "Galactorrhea") (in both men and women).[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

A patient with pituitary adenoma may present with [visual field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_field "Visual field") defects, classically on the left and right in [bitemporal hemianopsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitemporal_hemianopsia "Bitemporal hemianopsia"). It arises from the compression of the [optic nerve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optic_nerve "Optic nerve") by the tumor. The specific area of the visual pathway at which compression by these tumours occurs is at the [optic chiasm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optic_chiasm "Optic chiasm"). If originating superior to the optic chiasm, more commonly in a [craniopharyngioma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craniopharyngioma "Craniopharyngioma") of the [pituitary stalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_stalk "Pituitary stalk"), the visual field defect will first appear as [bitemporal inferior quadrantanopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrantanopia "Quadrantanopia"). If originating inferior to the optic chiasm the visual field defect will first appear as [bitemporal superior quadrantanopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrantanopia "Quadrantanopia"). Lateral expansion of a pituitary adenoma can also compress the [abducens nerve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abducens_nerve "Abducens nerve"), causing a [lateral rectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateral_rectus "Lateral rectus") palsy.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Acromegaly_and_Gigantism-9)

Also, a pituitary adenoma can cause symptoms of [increased intracranial pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intracranial_pressure#Increased_ICP "Intracranial pressure"). [Prolactinomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prolactinoma "Prolactinoma") often start to give symptoms especially during pregnancy, when the increased [hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormone "Hormone") level [estrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estrogen "Estrogen") can increase the tumor's growth rate.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-pmid26074878-10)

Various types of headaches are common in patients with pituitary adenomas. The adenoma may be the prime causative factor behind the headache or may serve to exacerbate a headache caused by other factors. Amongst the types of headaches experienced are both chronic and episodic [migraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migraine "Migraine"), and more uncommonly various unilateral headaches; primary stabbing headache,[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-11)[short-lasting unilateral neuralgiform headache attacks with conjunctival injection and tearing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short-lasting_unilateral_neuralgiform_headache_with_conjunctival_injection_and_tearing "Short-lasting unilateral neuralgiform headache with conjunctival injection and tearing") (SUNCT)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-12) – another type of stabbing headache characterized by short stabs of pain – [cluster headache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluster_headache "Cluster headache"),[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-13) and [hemicrania continua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemicrania_continua "Hemicrania continua") (HS).[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-14)

Compressive symptoms of pituitary adenomas (visual field deficits, decreased visual acuity, headaches) are more commonly seen with macroadenomas (which are greater than 10 mm in diameter) than with microadenomas (which are less than 10 mm in diameter).[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Melmed-15)

Non-secreting adenomas can go undetected for an extended time because no obvious abnormalities are seen; the gradual reduction in normal activities due to decreased production of hormones is rather less evident. For example, insufficient [adrenocorticotropic hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrenocorticotropic_hormone "Adrenocorticotropic hormone") means that the [adrenal glands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrenal_gland "Adrenal gland") will not produce sufficient [cortisol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cortisol "Cortisol"), resulting in slow recovery from illness, inflammation, and chronic fatigue; insufficient growth hormone in children and adolescents leads to diminished stature but which can have many other explanations.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

### Psychiatric

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pituitary_adenoma&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Psychiatric")]

Various psychiatric manifestations have been associated with pituitary disorders including pituitary adenomas. Psychiatric symptoms such as depression, anxiety[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-16) apathy, emotional instability, easy irritability and hostility have been noted.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-17)

### Complications

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pituitary_adenoma&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Complications")]

[![Image 5](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/10/Acromegaly_facial_features.JPEG/250px-Acromegaly_facial_features.JPEG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Acromegaly_facial_features.JPEG)

[Morphological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphology_(biology) "Morphology (biology)") facial changes caused by acromegaly; [frontal bossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontal_bossing "Frontal bossing"), enlarged nose, [prognathism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prognathism "Prognathism") and [maxillary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxilla "Maxilla") widening with separation of teeth and unseen, enlargement of the tongue, stuffy nose ([macroglossia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroglossia "Macroglossia"))

*   [Acromegaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acromegaly "Acromegaly") is a [syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syndrome "Syndrome") that results when the [anterior pituitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anterior_pituitary "Anterior pituitary") gland produces excess [growth hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_hormone "Growth hormone") (GH). Approximately 90–95% of acromegaly cases are caused by a pituitary adenoma and it most commonly affects middle aged adults,[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-18) Acromegly can result in severe disfigurement, serious complicating conditions, and premature death if unchecked. The disease which is often also associated with [gigantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigantism "Gigantism"), is difficult to diagnose in the early stages and is frequently missed for many years, until changes in external features, especially of the face, become noticeable with the median time from the development of initial symptoms to diagnosis being twelve years.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-19)
*   [Cushing's syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushing%27s_syndrome "Cushing's syndrome") is a hormonal disorder that causes hypercortisolism, which is elevated levels of [cortisol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cortisol "Cortisol") in the blood. [Cushing's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushing%27s_disease "Cushing's disease") (CD) is the most frequent cause of Cushing's syndrome, responsible for approximately 70% of cases.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-20) CD results when a pituitary adenoma causes excessive secretion of [adrenocorticotropic hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrenocorticotropic_hormone "Adrenocorticotropic hormone") (ACTH) that stimulates the [adrenal glands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrenal_glands "Adrenal glands") to produce excessive amounts of [cortisol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cortisol "Cortisol").[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-21)

Cushing's disease may cause fatigue, weight gain, fatty deposits around the abdomen and lower back (truncal obesity) and face ("moon face"), stretch marks ([striae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striae "Striae")) on the skin of the abdomen, thighs, breasts, and arms, [hypertension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertension "Hypertension"), [glucose intolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucose_intolerance "Glucose intolerance"), and various infections. In women, it may cause excessive growth of facial hair ([hirsutism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirsutism "Hirsutism")) and in men [erectile dysfunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erectile_dysfunction "Erectile dysfunction"). Psychiatric manifestations may include depression, [anxiety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anxiety "Anxiety"), easy irritability, and emotional instability. It may also result in various [cognitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognition "Cognition") difficulties.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
*   [Hyperpituitarism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperpituitarism "Hyperpituitarism") is a disease of the anterior lobe of the pituitary gland which is usually caused by a functional pituitary adenoma and results in hypersecretion of adenohypophyseal hormones such as growth hormone; prolactin; thyrotropin; luteinizing hormone; follicle-stimulating hormone; and adrenocorticotropic hormone.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
*   [Pituitary apoplexy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_apoplexy "Pituitary apoplexy") is a condition that occurs when pituitary adenomas suddenly hemorrhage internally, causing a rapid increase in size or when the tumor outgrows its blood supply which causes [tissue necrosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necrosis "Necrosis") and subsequent swelling of the dead tissue. Pituitary apoplexy often presents with visual loss and sudden onset headache and requires timely treatment often with [corticosteroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corticosteroids "Corticosteroids") and if necessary surgical intervention.[[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-22)
*   [Central diabetes insipidus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes_insipudus "Diabetes insipudus") is caused by diminished production of the [antidiuretic hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antidiuretic_hormone "Antidiuretic hormone")[vasopressin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasopressin "Vasopressin") that causes [severe thirst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polydipsia "Polydipsia") and excessive production of very dilute urine ([polyuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyuria "Polyuria")) which can lead to [dehydration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dehydration "Dehydration"). Vasopressin is produced in the [hypothalamus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus") and is then transported down the pituitary stalk and stored in the posterior lobe of the pituitary gland which then secretes it into the bloodstream.[[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-23)

As the pituitary gland is in close proximity to the brain, invasive adenomas may invade the [dura mater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dura_mater "Dura mater"), [cranial bone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_skull "Human skull"), or [sphenoid bone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphenoid_bone "Sphenoid bone").[[24]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-24)

## Risk factors

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pituitary_adenoma&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Risk factors")]

### Multiple endocrine neoplasia

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pituitary_adenoma&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Multiple endocrine neoplasia")]

Adenomas of the anterior pituitary gland are a major clinical feature of [multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_endocrine_neoplasia_type_1 "Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1") (MEN1), a rare inherited endocrine syndrome that affects 1 person in every 30,000. MEN causes various combinations of benign or malignant tumors in various glands in the endocrine system or may cause the glands to become [enlarged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperplasia "Hyperplasia") without forming tumors. It often affects the [parathyroid glands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parathyroid_gland "Parathyroid gland"), pancreatic islet cells, and anterior lobe of the pituitary gland. MEN1 may also cause non-endocrine tumors such as facial [angiofibromas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angiofibroma "Angiofibroma"), [collagenomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collagenoma "Collagenoma"), [lipomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipoma "Lipoma"), [meningiomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meningiomas "Meningiomas"), [ependymomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ependymoma "Ependymoma"), and [leiomyomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiomyomas "Leiomyomas"). Approximately 25 percent of patients with MEN1 develop pituitary adenomas.[[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-25)[[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-26)

### Carney complex

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pituitary_adenoma&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Carney complex")]

[Carney complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carney_complex "Carney complex") (CNC), also known as **LAMB syndrome**[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Schwartz20110502-27) and **NAME syndrome**[[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Schwartz20110502-27) is an [autosomal dominant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autosomal_dominant "Autosomal dominant") condition comprising [myxomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myxoma "Myxoma") of the heart and skin, hyperpigmentation of the skin ([lentiginosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lentiginosis "Lentiginosis")), and [endocrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocrine "Endocrine") overactivity and is distinct from [Carney's triad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carney%27s_triad "Carney's triad").[[28]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Carney1985-28)[[29]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-McCarthy1986-29) Approximately 7% of all cardiac myxomas are associated with Carney complex.[[30]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Reynen1995-30) Patients with CNC develop [growth hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_hormone "Growth hormone") (GH)-producing pituitary tumors and in some instances these same tumors also secrete [prolactin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prolactin "Prolactin"). There are however no isolated prolactinomas or any other type of pituitary tumor. In some patients with CNC, the pituitary gland is characterized by [hyperplastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperplasia "Hyperplasia") areas with the hyperplasia most likely preceding the formation of GH-producing adenomas.[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-31)

### Familial isolated pituitary adenoma

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pituitary_adenoma&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Familial isolated pituitary adenoma")]

Familial isolated pituitary adenoma (FIPA) is a term that is used to identify a condition that displays an [autosomal dominant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autosomal_dominant "Autosomal dominant") inheritance and is characterised by the presence of two or more related patients affected by adenomas of the pituitary gland only, with no other associated symptoms that occur in [multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_endocrine_neoplasia_type_1 "Multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1") (MEN-1), [Carney complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carney_complex "Carney complex") and with mutations in the aryl hydrocarbon receptor-interacting protein (AIP) gene.[[32]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-32)[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-:0-33)[[34]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-34) FIPA was first described in a limited cohort of families by [Albert Beckers](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Beckers "fr:Albert Beckers") group in Liège, Belgium;[[35]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-35) later FIPA was fully characterized in a multicenter international study of 64 families.[[33]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-:0-33) FIPA families are divided into those that are homogenous and have the same type of pituitary adenoma in all the affected family members (e.g. only [acromegaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acromegaly "Acromegaly"), only [prolactinoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prolactinoma "Prolactinoma"), etc.), while heterogeneous FIPA families can have different pituitary adenomas in affected family members.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-:1-36)

#### Genetics of FIPA

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pituitary_adenoma&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Genetics of FIPA")]

FIPA has two known genetic causes, [mutations in the AH receptor-interacting protein (AIP) gene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIP_gene_mutation "AIP gene mutation")[[37]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-37) and duplications in chromosome Xq26.3 that include the _[GPR101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPR101 "GPR101")_ gene that also causes X-linked acrogigantism (X-LAG) syndrome.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-:2-38) About 15–20% of FIPA families carry a germline _AIP_ gene mutation or deletion, and the disease occurs as autosomal dominant with incomplete penetrance, meaning that about 20% of AIP mutation carriers will develop a pituitary adenoma.[[36]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-:1-36)_AIP_ mutation associated pituitary adenomas (either presenting as FIPA or as individual, non familial cases) are usually growth hormone-secreting ([acromegaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acromegaly "Acromegaly")) or prolactin-secreting ([prolactinoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prolactinoma "Prolactinoma")) adenomas that are large (macroadenomas) and often occur in children, adolescents and young adults. Daly and colleagues showed that acromegaly cases with _AIP_ mutations occurred about 20 years before acromegaly cases without _AIP_ mutations and these tumors are large and relatively treatment-resistant.[[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-39) Due to their young age at onset, _AIP_ mutations are the most frequent genetic cause of [pituitary gigantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_gigantism "Pituitary gigantism") (29% of cases).[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-:4-40)

X-LAG is a rare syndrome of very early childhood onset pituitary tumors/hyperplasia that leads to growth hormone excess and severe overgrowth and pituitary gigantism.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-:2-38)[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-:3-41) Three FIPA families with X-LAG have been reported to date all of which had transmission of a chromosome Xq26.3 duplication from affected mother to affected son.[[41]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-:3-41)[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-:2-38) The disease characteristics of very young onset [pituitary gigantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_gigantism "Pituitary gigantism") leads to severe overgrowth if not treated adequately; many of the tallest humans in history (e.g. [Robert Pershing Wadlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wadlow "Robert Wadlow"); [Sandy Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy_Allen "Sandy Allen"), [André Rousimoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_the_Giant "André the Giant") (Andre the Giant), [Zeng Jinlian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeng_Jinlian "Zeng Jinlian")) had a similar clinical history to patients with X-LAG syndrome.[[42]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-42) The tallest historical individual with a known genetic cause was [Julius Koch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Koch "Julius Koch") (Geant Constantin) who was found to have X-LAG on genetic study of his skeleton.[[43]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-43) X-LAG has 100% penetrance so far (all affected with the Xq26.3 duplication have the disease and it affects predominantly females.[[38]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-:2-38) Isolated non familial cases of X-LAG can either have a constitutional duplication of a chromosome Xq26.3 including _[GPR101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPR101 "GPR101")_, or [mosaicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosaic_(genetics) "Mosaic (genetics)") for the duplication (present in a minority of cells) in the case of isolated male patients.[[44]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-44) X-LAG causes about 10% of cases of [pituitary gigantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigantism "Gigantism").[[40]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-:4-40)

## Mechanism

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pituitary_adenoma&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Mechanism")]

[![Image 6](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/Gray1180.png/250px-Gray1180.png)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Gray1180.png)

[Pituitary gland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_gland "Pituitary gland")

The pituitary gland or hypophysis is often referred to as the "master gland" of the human body. Part of the [hypothalamic-pituitary axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothalamic-pituitary_axis "Hypothalamic-pituitary axis"), it controls most of the body's [endocrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocrine "Endocrine") functions via the secretion of various [hormones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormone "Hormone") into the [circulatory system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circulatory_system "Circulatory system"). The pituitary gland is located below the brain in a depression ([fossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossa_(anatomy) "Fossa (anatomy)")) of the [sphenoid bone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphenoid_bone "Sphenoid bone") known as the [sella turcica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sella_turcica "Sella turcica"). Although anatomically and functionally connected to the brain, the pituitary gland[[45]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-45) sits outside the [blood–brain barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood%E2%80%93brain_barrier "Blood–brain barrier"). It is separated from the [subarachnoid space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarachnoid_space "Subarachnoid space") by the [diaphragma sella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaphragma_sella "Diaphragma sella"), therefore the [arachnoid mater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arachnoid_mater "Arachnoid mater") and thus [cerebral spinal fluid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebral_spinal_fluid "Cerebral spinal fluid") cannot enter the sella turcica.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

The pituitary gland is divided into two lobes, the anterior lobe (which accounts for two thirds of the volume of the gland), and the posterior lobe (one third of the volume) separated by the [pars intermedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pars_intermedia "Pars intermedia").[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov-8)

The [posterior lobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posterior_pituitary "Posterior pituitary") (the neural lobe or neurohypophysis) of the pituitary gland is not, despite its name, a true [gland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gland "Gland"). The posterior lobe contains [axons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axons "Axons") of [neurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurons "Neurons") that extend from the [hypothalamus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus") to which it is connected via the pituitary stalk. The hormones [vasopressin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasopressin "Vasopressin") and [oxytocin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxytocin "Oxytocin"), produced by the neurons of the [supraoptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supraoptic_nucleus "Supraoptic nucleus") and [paraventricular nuclei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraventricular_nuclei "Paraventricular nuclei") of the hypothalamus, are stored in the posterior lobe and released from axon endings ([dendrites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dendrites "Dendrites")) within the lobe.[[46]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-46)

The pituitary gland's [anterior lobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anterior_pituitary "Anterior pituitary") (adenohypophysis) is a true gland which produces and secretes six different hormones: [thyroid-stimulating hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyroid-stimulating_hormone "Thyroid-stimulating hormone") (TSH), [adrenocorticotropic hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrenocorticotropic_hormone "Adrenocorticotropic hormone") (ACTH), [follicle-stimulating hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Follicle-stimulating_hormone "Follicle-stimulating hormone") (FSH), [luteinizing hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luteinizing_hormone "Luteinizing hormone") (LH), [growth hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_hormone "Growth hormone") (GH), and [prolactin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prolactin "Prolactin") (PRL).[[47]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-47)

## Diagnosis

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pituitary_adenoma&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Diagnosis")]

Diagnosis of pituitary adenoma can be made, or at least suspected, by a constellation of related symptoms presented above.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Acromegaly_and_Gigantism-9)

The differential diagnosis includes pituitary tuberculoma, especially in developing countries and in immumocompromised patients.[[48]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-48) The diagnosis is confirmed by testing hormone levels, and by radiographic imaging of the pituitary (for example, by [CT scan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CT_scan "CT scan") or [MRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRI "MRI")).[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

### Classification

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pituitary_adenoma&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Classification")]

Pituitary adenomas are classified as endocrine tumors and also as brain tumors, though some patient groups prefer not to refer to them as brain tumors.[[49]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-49)[[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-50)[[51]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-51)[[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-52) Pituitary adenomas are classified based upon [anatomical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomy "Anatomy"), [histological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histology "Histology") and functional criteria.[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Ironside_JW_2003_561%E2%80%938-53)

*   Anatomically pituitary tumors are classified by their size based on radiological findings; either microadenomas (less than <10 mm) or macroadenomas (equal or greater than ≥10 mm).

Classification based on radioanatomical findings places adenomas into 1 of 4 grades (I–IV):[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-54)Stage I: microadenomas (<1 cm) without sella expansion.Stage II: macroadenomas (≥1 cm) and may extend above the sella.Stage III: macroadenomas with enlargement and invasion of the floor or suprasellar extension.Stage IV: destruction of the sella.
*   Histological classification utilizes an immunohistological characterization of the tumors in terms of their hormone production.[[53]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Ironside_JW_2003_561%E2%80%938-53) Historically they were classed as either [basophilic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basophilic "Basophilic"), [acidophilic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acidophile_(histology) "Acidophile (histology)"), or [chromophobic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromophobic "Chromophobic") on the basis of whether or not they took up the [tinctorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tincture "Tincture") stains [hematoxylin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hematoxylin "Hematoxylin") and [eosin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eosin "Eosin"). This classification has fallen into disuse, in favor of a classification based on what type of [hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormone "Hormone") is secreted by the tumor. Approximately 20–25% of adenomas do not secrete any readily identifiable active hormones ('non-functioning tumors') yet they are still sometimes referred to as 'chromophobic'.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
*   Functional classification is based upon the tumors endocrine activity as determined by [serum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serum_(blood) "Serum (blood)") hormone levels and pituitary tissue cellular hormone secretion detected via [immunohistochemical staining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunohistochemical_staining "Immunohistochemical staining").[[55]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-55) The "Percentage of hormone production cases" values are the fractions of adenomas producing each related hormone of each tumor type as compared to all cases of pituitary tumors, and does not directly correlate to the percentages of each tumor type because of lower or greater incidence of absence of secretion of the expected hormone. Thus, non secretive adenomas may be either _null cell adenomas_ or a more specific adenoma that, however, remains non-secretive.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
*   Any type of pituitary adenocarcinoma listed in the table below may cause compressive symptoms due to local expansion in addition to the systemic effects of secreted hormones listed in the pathology column.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]
*   Null cell adenomas by definition do not secrete hormones, but they commonly cause compressive effects on the [pituitary stalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_stalk "Pituitary stalk") (stalk effect). This leads to decreased levels of [dopamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopamine "Dopamine") from the hypothalamus reaching the anterior pituitary gland. Dopamine exerts an inhibitory effect on prolactin secretion. With the absence of this inhibitory effect, prolactin levels increase and are often increased in null cell adenomas. This leads to symptoms of hypogonadism.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Melmed-15)

#### Pituitary incidentalomas

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pituitary_adenoma&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Pituitary incidentalomas")]

Pituitary incidentalomas are pituitary tumors that are characterized as an [incidental finding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incidental_findings "Incidental findings"). They are often discovered by [computed tomography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computed_tomography "Computed tomography") (CT) or [magnetic resonance imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_resonance_imaging "Magnetic resonance imaging") (MRI), performed in the evaluation of unrelated medical conditions such as suspected [head trauma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_injury "Head injury"), in [cancer staging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_staging "Cancer staging") or in the evaluation of [nonspecific symptoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonspecific_symptom "Nonspecific symptom") such as [dizziness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizziness "Dizziness") and [headache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headache "Headache"). It is not uncommon for them to be discovered at [autopsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopsy "Autopsy"). In a [meta-analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta-analysis "Meta-analysis"), adenomas were found in an average of 16.7% in postmortem studies, with most being microadenomas (<10mm); macrodenomas accounted for only 0.16% to 0.2% of the decedents.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-pmid15274075-2) While non-secreting, noninvasive pituitary microadenomas are generally considered to be literally as well as [clinically benign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benign_tumor "Benign tumor"), a 2011 [meta-analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta-analysis "Meta-analysis") of available research showed there were, to that time, scant studies of low quality to support this assertion.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Balsells2011-3)

The Clinical Practice Guidelines, as published in April 2011 in _[The Journal of Clinical Endocrinology and Metabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Journal\_of\_Clinical\_Endocrinology\_and\_Metabolism "The Journal of Clinical Endocrinology and Metabolism")_ by the [Endocrine Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocrine_Society "Endocrine Society") (a professional, international medical organization), recommend that all patients with pituitary incidentalomas undergo a complete [medical history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_history "Medical history") and [physical examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_examination "Physical examination"), laboratory evaluations to screen for hormone hypersecretion and for [hypopituitarism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypopituitarism "Hypopituitarism"). If the lesion is in close proximity to the [optic nerves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optic_nerve "Optic nerve") or [optic chiasm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optic_chiasm "Optic chiasm"), a [visual field examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_field_test "Visual field test") should be performed. For those with incidentalomas which do not require surgical removal, follow up clinical assessments and neuroimaging should be performed as well follow-up visual field examinations for incidentalomas that abut or compress the optic nerve and chiasm and follow-up endocrine testing for macroincidentalomas.[[60]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-60)

#### Ectopic pituitary adenoma

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pituitary_adenoma&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Ectopic pituitary adenoma")]

An ectopic (occurring in an abnormal place) pituitary adenoma is a rare type of tumor which occurs outside of the [sella turcica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sella_turcica "Sella turcica"), most often in the [sphenoid sinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphenoid_sinus "Sphenoid sinus"),[[61]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-61) suprasellar region, [nasopharynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasopharynx "Nasopharynx") and the [cavernous sinuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavernous_sinus "Cavernous sinus").[[62]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-62)

#### Metastases to the pituitary gland

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pituitary_adenoma&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Metastases to the pituitary gland")]

Carcinomas that [metastasize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metastasis "Metastasis") into the pituitary gland are uncommon and typically seen in the elderly,[[63]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-63)[[64]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-64) with [lung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung_cancer "Lung cancer") and [breast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breast_cancer "Breast cancer") cancers being the most prevalent,[[65]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-65) In breast cancer patients, metastases to the pituitary gland occur in approximately 6–8% of cases.[[66]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-66)

Symptomatic pituitary metastases account for only 7% of reported cases. In those who are symptomatic [diabetes insipidus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes_insipidus "Diabetes insipidus") often occurs, with rates approximately 29–71%. Other commonly reported symptoms include anterior pituitary dysfunction, visual field defects, headache/pain, and [ophthalmoplegia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophthalmoplegia "Ophthalmoplegia").[[67]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-67)

## Treatment

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pituitary_adenoma&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Treatment")]

Treatment options depend on the type of tumor and on its size:

*   **[Prolactinomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prolactinoma "Prolactinoma")** (microadenomas and macroadenomas) are most often treated with [cabergoline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabergoline "Cabergoline") or [bromocriptine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bromocriptine "Bromocriptine") (both [dopamine agonists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopamine_agonists "Dopamine agonists")) as the first line of treatment.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Tritos_2023-68) Medical treatment usually effectively decreases tumor size as well as alleviates symptoms.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Tritos_2023-68) Pituitary adenomas are also followed by serial imaging (usually [MRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRI "MRI")) to detect any increase in size or mass effect on nearby structures. If medical therapy fails, the second option is usually [transphenoidal pituitary surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transsphenoidal_surgery "Transsphenoidal surgery").[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Tritos_2023-68) A third line therapy is [radiation therapy, proton therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton_therapy "Proton therapy") to shrink the tumor.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Tritos_2023-68)
*   **Thyrotropinomas** are treated with transsphenoidal pituitary surgery as the first line treatment option.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Tritos_2023-68) Medical therapy is a second line treatment, and thyrotropinomas respond to treatment with somatostatin receptor ligands such as [octreotide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octreotide "Octreotide") or [lanreotide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanreotide "Lanreotide").[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Tritos_2023-68) In people with thyrotropinomas, treatment with somatostatin receptor ligands normalized thyroid hormone levels in 80-90% of people, and 42% of people had a decreased tumor size.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Tritos_2023-68)
*   **Somatotrophic adenomas** are primarily treated with transsphenoidal pituitary surgery, especially if symptoms of acromegaly are present.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Tritos_2023-68) A second line treatment strategy (which can be used if there is a persistent or recurrent mass or symptoms after surgery) utilizes medical therapy, including [octreotide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octreotide "Octreotide") or [lanreotide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanreotide "Lanreotide"), which are long-acting [somatostatin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somatostatin "Somatostatin") analogs. These somatostatin receptor analogs inhibit secretion of growth hormone.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Tritos_2023-68) They were found to be about 50–55% effective in reducing tumor mass and reducing growth hormone and insulin like growth factor 1 ([IGF-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGF-1 "IGF-1")) levels in studies.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Melmed-15) The growth hormone receptor antagonist [pegvisomant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pegvisomant "Pegvisomant") is also used in the treatment of somatotrophic adenomas. Pegvisomant blocks the action of growth hormone. It can either be used as monotherapy or combined with a somatostatin analog.[[69]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Muhammad-69)
*   **Corticotropinomas** are primarily treated with transsphenoidal pituitary surgery, especially if signs and symptoms of [Cushing syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushing_syndrome "Cushing syndrome") are present.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Tritos_2023-68) Medication therapy is second line, and includes steroidogenesis inhibitors ([ketoconazole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketoconazole "Ketoconazole"), [metyrapone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metyrapone "Metyrapone"), [osilodrostat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osilodrostat "Osilodrostat"), [etomidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etomidate "Etomidate") or [mitotane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitotane "Mitotane")) which decrease production of cortisol, pituitary blockers such as somatostatin receptor ligand [pasireotide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasireotide "Pasireotide") or the dopamine agonist [cabergoline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabergoline "Cabergoline"), or the glucocorticoid receptor antagonist [mifepristone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mifepristone "Mifepristone").[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Tritos_2023-68) These medications can be combined for a synergistic effect. Medication therapy is often used in conjunction with radiation therapy for corticotropinomas.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Tritos_2023-68)
*   **Surgery** is a common treatment for pituitary tumors. The normal approach is [trans-sphenoidal adenectomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transsphenoidal_surgery "Transsphenoidal surgery"), which usually can remove the tumor without affecting the brain or optic nerves.[[70]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-70)
*   **Radiation** is also used to treat pituitary adenomas. Examples include external beam or proton beam radiation therapy or stereotactic radiosurgery. External radiation of pituitary adenomas can arrest tumor growth for several years but pituitary failure develops within 10 years in most patients necessitating lifelong hormone replacement.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Melmed-15) Radiation therapy for pituitary adenomas is associated with a four-fold increase in mortality due to cerebrovascular disease.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Melmed-15) Lifelong monitoring of pituitary hormones is recommended after radiation therapy as [hypopituitarism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypopituitarism "Hypopituitarism") developed in 17% of those undergoing radiation therapy.[[68]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary_adenoma#cite_note-Tritos_2023-68)
